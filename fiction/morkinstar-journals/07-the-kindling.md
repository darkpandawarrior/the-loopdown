# The Kindling

> Entry #2284 · Series 3 of 7, Cendrewake System

Greetings again, my dear readers. Lu'kifær Morkinstar. Journal Entry #2284, third of seven from Cendrewake.

I am going to tell you up front that this is not a comfortable entry and that I have rewritten the end of it four times. You may want to save it for a day when you have somewhere to be afterwards.

Cendre is the second planet of Cendrewake, a small cool orange star with a large and boring family of worlds. Cendre itself is temperate, forested, unremarkable in every geological respect, and its people, whom the Directory has filed as "Cendran" and who call themselves **Væhn**, are Category 3 and have been Category 3 for a very long time.

That last clause is the entry.

The Væhn have had a written language for something in the region of four thousand clicks. They have had metallurgy, agriculture, medicine, formal mathematics, and a genuinely sophisticated theory of optics for most of that. And they have never once had an industrial revolution, or spaceflight, or in fact any of the discontinuities the Directory's development models say a species of their profile should have hit six or seven times by now.

The Directory's assessment, filed 30 galaxals ago and never revisited, is *culturally conservative; low innovation drive*.

The Directory's assessment is, once again, a first impression written by somebody who did not ask.

Here is what actually happens on Cendre. Every ninth generation, the Væhn burn everything they have ever written.

Everything. Not a symbolic bonfire with a few scrolls in it. The entire written output of nine generations of a global civilisation: libraries, land deeds, medical texts, star charts, letters, tax records, the optics, the mathematics, the lot. They spend the better part of a generation *preparing* for it, cataloguing and consolidating so the burn is complete. They call it the **Kindling**. It has happened, by their count, ninety-one times.

Their self-term, **Væhn**, translates as "those who begin".

Now. The legend, which I promise you explains it, and then the part after the legend, which is why I am not sleeping well.

Many milgalaxals ago, in the age the Væhn call the First Weight, the world of Cendre was held between 14 gods and 14 monsters.

Seventh world. Fifth system. You know the drill by now and I am past the point of pretending to be surprised. (My editor keeps asking if I get a bonus per halving. I do not.)

The god is **Anh-Rekk**, keeper of memory, and by every account the most beloved figure in the Væhn pantheon, which is going to matter shortly.

The monster is **Skerrin**. The Tally.

Skerrin has no body. That is the first thing every rendition says and it is the thing that makes this legend different from every other one I have collected in this series. K'öæluæ had scales. Uhl had a mouth. Grin had whatever face it had taken that morning. Skerrin has nothing of its own at all.

Skerrin's body is the record.

Every mark ever made on Cendre is a part of Skerrin. Every tally, every deed, every love letter, every shopping list. When a Væhn writes something down, Skerrin gets bigger by exactly that much, and there is no way to write something down that does not do this, and Skerrin does not care what the mark says. It is not reading. It is accumulating.

And Skerrin is not malicious. I want to stress this because I have now filed seven of these and the Væhn are the fourth culture in a row to go out of their way to tell me their monster meant no harm, and I no longer think that is a coincidence about monsters. I think it is a fact about the kind of people who survive long enough to have a legend.

Skerrin is a consequence.

What happens when Skerrin gets heavy is the part I had to have explained to me three times, because I kept trying to make it a physical thing and it is not.

The weight of Skerrin is **precedent**.

A Væhn scholar put it to me like this. When your record is small, a question is a thing you have to go and answer. When your record is large, a question is a thing you look up. And when your record is *complete enough*, every question anyone thinks to ask has already been answered by someone better resourced and longer dead, and the correct, rational, efficient move in every single case is to go and find their answer instead of making your own.

Nobody decides to stop. Everybody, individually, makes the sensible choice.

The Væhn have a term for the end state of that process, and the rig gives it to me as **the Still**, and their histories describe the last one, which ran for a little over two hundred clicks before the ninety-first Kindling, in language that I found harder to read than anything I have filed from Jötunheimr.

Nobody died in the Still. There was no famine, no war, no plague, and no tyranny. Crop yields were good. The optics were excellent. And nothing happened. For two hundred clicks, on an entire planet, nothing happened, because everything had an entry, and a people who can look up the answer to everything eventually stop being a people who have answers.

Anh-Rekk, keeper of memory, is the one who ended it.

He split himself in half.

(Seventh legend, seventh halving, and at this point I am simply reporting the pattern without comment because you can all see it as well as I can.)

One half of Anh-Rekk became the fire. Not a metaphorical fire. The Væhn light every Kindling from a single flame kept burning between cycles in the capital, and their claim is that it is the same fire, and I have no way to check that and no particular desire to.

The other half of Anh-Rekk went into the thing they keep.

Because they do not burn all of it, my dear readers. I said they burn everything and I want to correct myself the way I corrected myself about the Marltains, because the exception is the entire point and I have been building to it.

**They keep one page.**

One. Per Kindling. Written after the fire is out, on the morning of the first day of the new count, by a child. Not a scholar, not a priest, not the oldest and wisest. A child, chosen by lot, roughly six or seven years old, who has been alive for the burn and remembers it and cannot possibly understand it.

And there is exactly one rule about what may go on that page.

It must be something that has never been written down before.

Not the best thing. Not the most important thing. Something *new*. A child, on a planet that has just deleted itself, holding a stylus, being asked for one thing nobody has ever said.

The complete surviving archive of the Væhn civilisation, four thousand clicks of it, is therefore ninety-one pages, in a wooden case, in a room with a window, guarded by nobody.

[Fun Cendre Fact #2- The case is not locked. I asked. The custodian was baffled by the question and eventually said that a lock would imply somebody might want to take it, and that anyone who wanted it that badly should probably have it.]

They let me read them.

I am going to tell you about that carefully, because it is the last thing in this entry and I have taken four attempts at it.

The custodian brought the case out and put it on a table by the window and left the room, which I understand now was a kindness and did not at the time. Ninety-one pages, in ninety-one hands, the oldest so soft with age it has been sleeved in something like glass.

They are short. That was the first surprise. Most of them are one or two lines. A few are a single word. Two of them are drawings.

They are not profound. That was the second surprise and it took me a while to stop being disappointed by it. There is no wisdom in that case. There is a page that says, as far as the rig can render it, *the small birds come back before the big ones and nobody had said so*. There is a page that appears to be a joke, and the custodian confirmed it is a joke, and told me it is still funny and then told it to me and it is still funny. There is a page with a list of four names on it and nothing else, and the note underneath in a much later hand explaining that nobody now knows who they were, which is the whole reason the page still matters.

Ninety-one children. Ninety-one things nobody had ever bothered to write down. That is the entire inheritance of a civilisation and they have kept it in a wooden box by a window for four thousand clicks and they will burn everything else again in about ninety clicks' time and they are not frightened of it.

I want to stop here and tell you something, because it happened at that table and not anywhere more dramatic, and I would rather you heard it from me now than had to guess later that I noticed and said nothing.

Since Entry #2263 I have been carrying an explanation for the fourteen and the fourteen. I never gave it much of a name in front of you, but I have one for it in my own head: the Template Hypothesis. The number is not a fact about the worlds. It is a fact about the paperwork. Either some surveyor forty galaxals back filled in a form with fourteen slots for gods and fourteen for monsters, and every field officer since has copied it forward without asking, the way the Killugan hold sat filed under Greetings, physical for forty galaxals and nobody thought to look again. Or it is my own rig, quietly rounding whatever it hears into a shape I would recognise, because a machine built to translate for a man has every incentive to hand that man something familiar. The Marlt binary, the Killugan hold, the Vaehn assessment I already gave you at the top of this entry, and I am still not touching the milgalaxal conversion. A season of Directory sloppiness, and I built a theory the same shape as the sloppiness, and I built it honestly.

It does not survive Cendre, and it does not fail to survive by accident. A theory about paperwork needs paperwork: a form sitting in a drawer somewhere, uncorrected, long enough to get copied forward by people who never check it, something old enough to have quietly become tradition. Cendre keeps nothing that old. Cendre keeps nothing older than the last Kindling, on purpose, because the entire civilisation spends a generation making sure of it and then sets fire to the result. There is no Directory-era document on this planet for a classification form to leak into. There is no continuous written record here for a rig to have been quietly rounding, generation over generation, because there is no continuous written record here, full stop. There is a box with ninety-one pages in it, and the count is not hiding in any of them by accident. It is sitting on the oldest one, written by a child on the first morning after the first Kindling, kept unlocked in a room with a window for four thousand clicks, on purpose, across ninety-one separate burnings, by a people whose entire cultural project is deciding what is worth keeping when everything else goes.

I have had one explanation since Entry #2263. I sat at that table and watched the one planet in this whole series that could not possibly have gotten the number from a form or a rig report the number back to me anyway, and I did not have a second explanation ready. I still do not.

The oldest page has one word on it.

I have been asking the same question on every world in this series: name the fourteen monsters. Six worlds, six times thirteen names, six pauses, and I did not have to ask on Cendre.

The custodian came back in while I was still holding the first page, and saw where my hands were, and told me what it was. She said it plainly and without ceremony, the way you would tell a guest which tap is the hot one.

She said: that is the name of the fourteenth. A child wrote it, at the first Kindling, because it was the one thing nobody had ever written down.

And that is why they burn everything, my dear readers. It is not conservatism and it is not a low innovation drive and I would like the Directory to note that in the file. It is that they worked out, four thousand clicks ago, that the fourteenth monster's body is made of writing, and that you cannot fight a thing whose mass is your own record, and that the only move available to a people in that position is to periodically become light again.

Skerrin is not the fourteenth. Skerrin is the thirteenth. I checked. Skerrin is local, Skerrin is theirs, Skerrin is on the Cendran list with a name on it like the other twelve.

The fourteenth is the one the child wrote, and it is not from Cendre, and the Væhn are the only people in five systems who have it written down anywhere, and they keep it in an unlocked box.

I read it.

I have not written it here.

I want to be honest with you about why, because I have spent 2284 entries insisting that my job is to report and not to judge, and this is the first time in all of them that I have held something back, and you are owed the reason.

It is not that I am protecting you. I am not that noble and you would see through it.

It is that I looked at that word for about four seconds and then I closed the case, and I have not put it in my logbook, and the reason is that my logbook is the thing I open every single day of my life, and I do not want to see it in there.

That is all. That is the entire reason. A grown correspondent with 2284 entries and accreditation from the Galactic Directory, sitting in a room with a window, deciding not to write something down because he did not want to have to look at it again.

Which, I notice, is precisely and exactly the Væhn's own argument, arrived at independently, in four seconds, by someone who came here to explain them.

[There is no third Fun Cendre Fact in this entry. I had one. It was about the ink. I have taken it out.]

Is there truth in their legend? No one knows. My aim is never to prove or disprove the tales I come across. Just to report them to you, and apparently, now, to occasionally not.

This is Lu'kifær Morkinstar concluding Journal Entry #2284, Series 3 of 7 from the Cendrewake System.
