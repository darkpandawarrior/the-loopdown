# RULING: THE REGISTER, FORWARD ONLY

## The fourth lens is upheld in law and overruled in scope

Proposal four's charge is correct and becomes the constitution of the design: a site that cross-references everything and understands nothing is the Galactic Directory, and a footer reading "this entry demonstrates Law V" is the Template Hypothesis in HTML. Its two laws are adopted verbatim and every edge below obeys them:

**Law A — the register runs forward in time only.** A page may carry what later happened to it. Nothing may hand back what was destroyed, and nothing may point at a meaning. Events may be recorded; meanings may never be. This is "a retroaction may change what a season meant, never what it cost" transposed to the link graph, and it is why the fire never links back to the page it burned.

**Law B — the register is corpus state, never reader state.** Every line is true for everyone the moment a season ships. No localStorage, no visited styling, no progress, no resume. The puzzle box becomes structurally impossible rather than merely discouraged, and the whole thing stays static HTML.

But the fourth lens's conclusion — near-total refusal — over-corrects. The Directory's crime is cross-referencing *without checking*. A register whose every line is generated from a fact the corpus actually states, with a test pinning the join, is the archive the Directory never was. That is a quiet joke the site earns by construction and must never state.

## The Damage Register: the one new component

A `<footer class="notice-conditions">` on /read/$slug, outside `.piece-body` so no scraper can ingest it, rendered after the last line of prose and before the season list. Mono, uppercase kicker `NOTICE-CONDITIONS`, body at Terminologies size, `--color-muted`, no border, no card, no icon, no heading besides the kicker, hard cap of four lines, fixed order (fate · world · teller · filing). A line renders only when its fact is true of that record; otherwise it is absent, because absence is the tell (Season Two's `chrome: "none"` is already this doctrine). No "none recorded" placeholder — ten identical empty labels on Season One is a bet on a season that does not exist.

**The widget guard, mechanical not aspirational:** a test asserting (1) no register line's text begins with a verb addressed to the reader — no "read", "see", "explore"; every line's grammatical subject is the record; (2) the line count never exceeds four; (3) the thirteen kindling-to-page joins match a pinned fixture, and the join is scoped so the ten S1 entries carrying `page 0` never match (P1 found this live landmine: an unscoped join stamps a withdrawal line across all of Season One). The register that cannot be tested is the register that becomes a link farm in a mono font.

## Edges that ship

1. **S2 record → the Kindling piece that withdrew it.** `page 1 withdrawn · Kindling 1`. The fate is the link, whole, underline on hover only. Fable's own named mechanism, exact canon wording, forward in time. Appears only on records whose burning the shipped S3 data attests; the pages without a shipped fate carry no line and nothing explains why. This line, sitting at the foot of the first page of the case — the first thing every Season Two reader reads — is the trapdoor: it moves a reader across a season boundary with the boundary doing all the work.

2. **S3 piece → the record it consumed, AS TEXT ONLY.** `page 1 · withdrawn from the case`. Never a link, including the eight the reader could reach. The plate already carries the damaged remnant; it stays a picture. An anchor from the ash back to the intact page refunds the fire, and the fire's cost is the one non-negotiable thing in this anthology. The five that resolve to pages never shipped are the same line with a number no route answers, and they stay that way forever: they teach, wordlessly, that some of this is permanently out of reach.

3. **Record → its world on the map, at a stated count.** `Vœrhan · concluded at 615`, the number the link, to `/anthology?layer=map&world=Vœrhan&at=615`. Only where the entry's own text or the shipped starmap data states the count — no interpolation, no inference. The slider is already at position on arrival; nothing animates, ever. Entries with no world get no line: a man between two systems he will not name has no catalogue reference. Prerequisite, and the only routing work in the design: `/anthology`'s tab `useState` becomes search-param state (`layer=form|case|fire|map|tellers`, never numbers) — /canon's own docstring already made this argument.

4. **Record → its teller's slot on the roll.** The teller aside that already exists at the foot becomes the link — the name, not a new line, no new furniture — to the roll anchored on that card. Where the entry has an argued absence, the aside links to the blank card that argues it. Law five is only checkable if the absences are visible from inside the entry. P4's objection (catalogue-row conversion) is noted and answered by what the roll becomes in edge 5.

5. **Teller → every page they tell in.** The `keysOf` first-match-wins defect is fixed at the shared helper: a teller's card lists each of their pages as plain small-print rows. This is a bug fix wearing a design's clothes — the shipped site asserts a false thing about its own subject, which is the Directory's defining failure reproduced in the archive documenting it. Also fix upstream: the generator ships 10 of 20 witnesses and misfiles Ossul; edge 5 is not done until `gen-anthology.mjs` is.

6. **Canon law → the entry where it fires.** Unchanged, one direction only, exactly as shipped. A reference desk citing where a rule was written down is what a reference desk is for. The canonLore slug test already guards it.

7. **Kicker → the collection.** The pill button ("Back to The Morkinstar Journals", book icon) is deleted — it is the one link card on the reading page and the site asking a stranger to leave before reading a sentence. The season title inside the kicker line becomes the link instead. A card dies; no information is lost. This is the single highest-leverage change in the brief and it is a deletion.

8. **#2300 and the kept page end.** The "More from this season" list is removed from these two only. #2300 ends at ENTRY FILED INCOMPLETE; the kept page ends at blank paper; below either, the site footer and nothing else. And the guard becomes a test, not a comment: og:description on #2300 is asserted to equal the authored blurb, its body's final characters asserted untouched, and `gen-anthology.mjs`'s `e.blurb || ""` is pinned so no future body-excerpt fallback can delete the ending of the anthology.

## Edges refused, with reasons

- **Entry → the law it turns on.** The Template Hypothesis executed in HTML: pressing a category onto every page is how the Killugan hold got filed as *Greetings, physical*. Canon cites its subjects; the subjects do not cite the institution back. That asymmetry IS the canon.
- **Entry → the seasons that reframe it, in any wording.** A retroaction is not a destination. "Reframed by Season Three" performs the interloop, which my own ruling prohibits twice. Events ship; meanings never do.
- **S3 → S2 as a link.** Law A. The ash does not hand the page back.
- **The rendering word (`held / strained / failed / refused`) in entry chrome.** Two proposals loved it; it is a meaning-line in a one-word disguise — it exists to reprice every portrait the reader has accepted, which is a reframe surfaced by chrome. The Rendering lives on /canon, where a reader chose to walk into the doctrine. Refused with regret, which is how you know the refusal is real.
- **Doctrine-wide canon citations, teller names on starmap labels, unseen burned worlds as pins, full-text search, tag pages, breadcrumbs, related-anything, any similarity computation, any reader state, any second appearance of the flat line, any animated counter, any "none recorded" placeholder, alt text on plates.** Each is either reader-state, a meaning-line, an invented world, or furniture. The site may never compute a relationship the fiction has not written down.
- **Any Season Four edge.** Retroaction-first design is a retcon with better manners. S4 wiring is authored when S4 pages exist.

## The four objects

`/anthology` with no `layer` param is the wall, bare: title, premise, and the one flat sentence — "works separately, together, or in any order" — said here and nowhere else, ever. The layer switches are named the form, the case, the fire, the map, the tellers — never numbers — as `<button aria-pressed>` addressable by URL. The torn-edge visual treatment (earlier chrome visible at the strips) is approved as craft but staged BEHIND the register and behind the 1.00 accessibility gate and a 360px check: if the wall never ships its tear, the design still stands; if the register never ships, nothing does. The strips carry no prose; the moment a strip grows a sentence it is a banner and is cut. Publication order remains the default first-visit path.

## The stumbler

She lands on /read/the-second-chair from a search for something else. Above the fold: kicker (`THE NINETY-ONE PAGES · PAGE 1 OF 91`), title, sigil, word count, plate. In three seconds she knows she is inside a numbered record and not on a blog, and nobody told her either thing — the numbering is the orientation, so no welcome mat is offered. Nothing above the fold is clickable except the kicker's season title. If she leaves, the design cost her nothing and taught her nothing false. If she reaches the foot: the teller, a person with a face who did one specific thing — the object that converts a stranger, already built — and then two or three lines of small print she was not asked to notice. One of them says what later happened to the page she just read.

## Consumption, honestly

Thirty-four pieces is roughly four hours of prose, so "consumed" means "they read the anthology," and the prose does that work, not the wiring. The wiring's entire job is to remove reasons to stop: the existing season list carries eighty per cent of continuation and is not touched; the trapdoor at the foot of the case's first page fires once per reader and moves them across a season boundary irreversibly, because there is no link back; the map is the loiter surface that hands them back to prose; and the dead lines are stops, which are part of being consumed — a reader who sits four seconds with a line that goes nowhere is further inside this thing than one who clicks through. Variance is the only teacher: registers are different lengths, some lines link, some refuse, and no copy explains any of it, which is how the anthology teaches everything else. The honest limit, stated rather than designed around: this pulls people who read; a skimmer bounces, because every instrument that would hold one — progress, unlocks, streaks, an animated counter — is refused. That is the correct trade. A machine that manufactures four hours out of one entry cannot be built here without becoming the Directory. What ships instead is a link graph that never lies about what it cost.

## The anti-puzzle-box guarantee, literally

Every register line is corpus-true, forward-facing, and skippable. A reader who reads thirty-four pieces in publication order and never touches a register meets every retroaction in the prose, where they were always planted, and misses nothing. No line appears because a prerequisite was met; no path opens what another closes; no accumulation of edges resolves whether the fire worked, who the readers are, what the fourteenth is called, or what he looks like. If a reader who follows every line can assemble an answer a reader who follows none cannot, the wiring has failed at the only thing it is for — and the register's test suite is where that claim stops being an intention encoded correctly and executed into nothing.

**Build order:** (1) URL-addressable layers; (2) the register with its join-fixture and grammar tests; (3) the pill deletion and kicker link; (4) the `keysOf`/generator fix; (5) the #2300 guards; (6) the two terminal pages; (7) the wall's torn edges, gated. Items 1–6 ship together or the reciprocity claims above are prose.
