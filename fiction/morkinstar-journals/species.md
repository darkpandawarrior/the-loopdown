# The Species Bible

## The unlock

The first portraits came back as ten nineteenth-century European humans. That was my prompting,
not the model. But fixing it by drawing *slightly stranger people* would have been the smaller
mistake made more carefully, so here is the actual answer, and canon hands it over.

**We have never seen anyone in this anthology.**

Everything across thirty entries arrived through a **translation rig**: a device that "renders",
that Morkinstar argues with, that "fights him on that for two days", whose renderings vary between
tellings and occasionally fail outright. And every description was then written onto a **Galactic
Directory form**, by an institution whose defining trait for sixty galaxals has been pressing a
familiar shape onto whatever it files.

"Bipedal category species" is not an observation. **It is a checkbox.**

The Template Hypothesis was the correspondent's great wrong idea: that the Directory printed the
count of fourteen onto worlds that never had it. He was wrong about the number. He was wrong
because he tested it on the *gods*.

**He never tested it on the bodies.**

So the portraits are not photographs and were never claiming to be. They are **renderings**, and
the doctrine of this bible is that a rendering can hold, or strain, or fail, or refuse. That
variance is not inconsistency. It is the most honest thing in the art.

## What this permits, which is everything

- A plate may be legible and warm, and that means the rig held.
- A plate may be a figure half-resolved into what it actually is, and that means the rig strained.
- A plate may be mostly absence with a pair of hands in it, and that means the rig failed.
- **One plate may be a refusal**, and that is canon: Ossul's species is never named anywhere in
  thirty entries, so the rig does not get to render him.

Human, non-human, and the blur between are all available and all mean something specific now.
A figure that reads humanlike is not a design failure. It is a rendering that resolved toward the
familiar, which is exactly what a Directory instrument would do, and the reader can distrust it.

## The four constraints that survive

These are explicit sentences and the rig cannot render them away.

| Species | World | Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| **Vöskh** | Grïnjdarlay | Auditory organ **runs the length of the jawline**. Tveggi was born without it. |
| **Marltains** | Marlt | **More than two sexes. Obligate twin birth, always.** |
| **Ombri** | Killuga Var | Resting temperature **high enough to read as fever**. |
| **Hraedh** | Jötunheimr | **Enormous.** |

Canon is silent on eight of fourteen species. Silence is permission.

## The unexploited fact that should have changed everything

> *"K'öæluæ and Feeriko had offspring of their own."*

The Samuins carry the blood of a fire serpent who split himself in half and burned both halves,
and whose scales fall on that world as snow every click. **Feeriko's descendants are god-descended
and I drew her in a parka.**

Every world in this series had a god in it, most of them recently, several of them intimately.
Draw that.

## The rule that keeps it from becoming monster art

The failure that would break the series is not weirdness. It is a plate reading as a **cool
creature design** rather than as a frightened child or a tired teacher. The thesis is *the heroes
lose and the tellers are why there is a story at all.* If the alienness stops serving the person,
it has failed even if it is beautiful.

The guard, and it comes from the text itself: **the corpus says hand or hands 63 times.** Every
other body word combined is about twenty. `fur` and `limb` appear zero times. These stories have
always been about hands, and a pair of hands doing careful work reads as a person no matter what
it is attached to, at any scale, in any medium.

**So: however cosmic the plate goes, the hands stay legible and stay busy.** That is the tether.

Emotion is carried by posture, tension and direction of attention. **Not by eyes.** Changing eye
count is the cheapest and most overused alien signal there is and it produces exactly the
rubber-forehead result we are escaping. Several of these have no face at all.

## The ten renderings

Scale is a free variable now. Some of these are a person. Some are a person and a world in the
same frame.

| # | Teller | The rendering | Canon it is made of |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Feeriko** | God-blooded. Ice-light under the skin, scales surfacing at the forearms where the cold bit her, snow falling *out* of her rather than onto her. Hands raised mid-sentence, entirely legible. | She married the serpent and bore his children. The snow is his scales. |
| 2 | **Tveggi** | The only still thing in a screaming world. Everything around her smeared with vibration; she is drawn in absolute, silent stillness. The jaw-fringe every other Vöskh has is smooth on her. The mark she cuts is the one bright thing. | Born without the organ. Deafness is why she could keep the count. |

| 5 | **Ædri** | Two figures in an embrace. She is drawn in ink and heat-bloom, forearm flanges spread. **The one she is holding is drawn in nothing at all.** | Runs hot. Eleven. The cold one is still out there. |
| 6 | **Hild-Ronn** | Rendered at the scale of the ridge. The line of upright graves recedes to the horizon behind her, four billion, each aimed. Her hands, enormous and blunt but for one fine digit, set a stone. | Enormous. Stonework to a third of a degree. |

## Prompting

**Never use:** woman, man, clerk, child, wearing, face, eyes, smiling, parka, coat, hat,
spectacles, elderly, bearded, or any period or nationality.

**Skeleton:** silhouette → what the body is made of → the one divergence → the hands and their
task → scale and environment → style → negatives.

Style stays: ink and wash, crosshatch, aged paper, single muted tone. It is working, it is the
field-journal frame, and it is what makes the cosmic material land instead of looking like
concept art. **Restraint in the medium is what buys permission in the subject.**

Always negative: *not human, no human clothing, no period costume, no lettering.*

## The prose follow-up

The narrator should not start describing anatomy. He does not find his hosts remarkable and that
voice is load-bearing. What changes is **physical interaction**: not *"he handed me the page"* but
*"he slid the page across with the flat of his lower jaw."* He does not comment on the jaw. He
just uses it. The bodies stop being invisible without the field report becoming a biology lecture.
