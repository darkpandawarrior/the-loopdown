# The Cold Case Of All Fourteen

> Page 9 of 91

Greetings again, my dear readers. Lu'kifær Morkinstar. Page nine, and I want to say upfront that this is the first page in the case where I got handed something instead of going and finding it, which should have made it easier and instead made it the hardest one so far.

Dhurin is a mid category three world, temperate, unremarkable star, the kind of place I would ordinarily be through in eleven days and gone. The Directory has it filed as home to the "Dhurinae." Nobody on Dhurin has ever called themselves that. Their own word is **Kest**, which the rig gives me straight and does not soften at all: *the ones who have not closed it.* I filed the correction. I have stopped expecting anything to happen to a correction and I filed it anyway, because that is apparently a thing I still do.

And here is the situation, stated the way they would state it to me, which took some getting used to.

Four thousand clicks ago, in a single night, all fourteen gods of Dhurin died. And all fourteen monsters died with them. Twenty eight, gone, same night, no war recorded, no invader, no plague, no weather event on any instrument a Category 3 world could have had running at the time. One night everything that had ever been a god or a monster on Dhurin was alive. The next morning none of it was, and it has stayed that way for four thousand clicks, and nobody on Dhurin has ever agreed on why.

Which on any other world would be the whole legend, filed, done, on to the next planet. On Dhurin it is not a legend. It is an open case. And the entire civilisation, near as I can tell, is built around not closing it.

Here is how that works, because I want you to sit with it the way I had to.

Their priests are not priests. They are Examiners, and ordination is not a rite, it is a bar exam. A Kest child who wants the collar has to stand in front of three sitting Examiners and defend a piece of evidence from cross examination for as long as they can keep their footing, and I am told the record is eleven days and the man who set it retired shortly afterward on the grounds that he had nothing left to prove to anybody.

Their scripture is case files. Actual case files, in an actual citation format, and I had one read out to me until I could transcribe it: *Dhurin Case 000042, Exhibit C, disproven per Examiner Voss'kel, Day 1,140,206.* That is a hymn on Dhurin. People recite that.

Their calendar does not count years. It counts days since, and since is always the same since. Day one is the morning after. Everything the Kest have ever written is dated forward from a night none of them were alive for, and none of them have ever reset the count, not once, in four thousand clicks, which by my own math comes out to somewhere past two million nine hundred thousand days, and when I said that number out loud to the Examiner who was walking me through it she looked at me the way you look at someone who has just converted a grief into a unit of measurement for no reason anyone asked for. Which, fair.

Their highest honour does not go to whoever names a suspect. It goes to whoever clears one. The title is Clearing Hand, it has been awarded four times in four thousand clicks, and each time it went to an Examiner who spent a working life proving that one specific god or monster could not have killed the others, which you would think narrows nothing since none of the twenty eight could plausibly have murdered themselves, and you would be right, and the Kest know you would be right, and they give the honour anyway, because on Dhurin eliminating a wrong answer counts as work and proposing one does not.

And then there is the part that undid me a little.

They keep everything they have ever disproven. All of it. Four thousand clicks of wrong theories, filed, indexed, preserved, and it is enormous, and they are proud of it in a way I have not seen a civilisation be proud of anything else I have catalogued in this case. A Kest child is taught the wrong answers first. Before the case itself, before the twenty eight names, you learn the whole shelf of things it wasn't, in order, so that by the time anyone tells you what is still standing you already know everything it isn't standing on.

[Page Fact- I asked an Examiner what happens to a theory once it is disproven and filed. She said nothing happens to it. It stays exactly where it is, forever, being wrong in public. I asked if that ever felt like a punishment. She said it was the opposite, it was the only way anybody ever got to be finished with anything on Dhurin, and then she went back to her filing before I could ask what she meant, which I now think was deliberate.]

I was handed the file itself on the ninth day, and here is why, since I did not simply walk in and ask for it. The Kest have a standing rule that any case older than a million days is required, by their own procedure, to be reviewed at intervals by somebody with no stake in the answer and a demonstrated habit of keeping records straight. Word had reached them, through channels I have not traced and would rather not, that I currently qualified. I have a wooden case with nine pages in it as of this writing and apparently that is now a credential.

So. The evidence, such as it is, and I want to give you two pieces of it because they are the two that moved me.

The first is the timing. Every one of the twenty eight has its own case file, its own Examiner of record going back generations, its own chain of testimony, because a god who dies gets investigated exactly like anyone else on Dhurin, which is either the most respectful thing I have ever seen a religion do or the least religious thing I have ever seen a religion do, and I am no longer sure those are different. And every one of those twenty eight files, kept independently, by different Examiners, in different regions, across four thousand clicks of nobody comparing notes on purpose because comparing notes on purpose is against procedure, converges. Same night. Not same season, not same year margin of error. Twenty eight independent chains of custody, and they land on one night, every time anybody checks, and the Kest check constantly, because checking is the entire religion.

Even the case file gets to thirteen names on the monster side and then does the thing every world in this series does. Not a taboo. A citation. The fourteenth line reads *Suspect, unnamed, cross referenced under case number only,* because you cannot indict a name nobody has, and the Kest, being the most rigorous people I have met, filed that limitation honestly instead of inventing something to fill the blank. I have seen six worlds flinch from that name. I have never before seen one file the flinch as evidence.

The second piece is the one that got under me. Their library of disproven theories, the enormous proud one, is arranged as a single unbroken shelf, oldest to newest, and every entry on it, every single one across four thousand clicks, is logged the same dry way: theory, proposed, disproven, citation, next. Except the very last entry before the night in question. That one is not logged as disproven. It is logged, in the same hand, in the same format, as *resolved,* with no citation after it, because there was no Examiner left alive the next morning to write one.

Nobody knows what the question was. The word survives. The answer does not.

I put it together out loud, in the Examiner's office, and I want to be honest that I did not mean to. I said something like, what if the last open question on Dhurin got answered that night, and there was nothing left the world didn't know, and the fourteen just went out the way a held breath goes out once you finally, actually know.

She let me finish. Then she told me, not unkindly, that a theory is not evidence and evidence is not a career, and that proposing an answer on Dhurin earns you exactly nothing, which she was saying to remind me, I think, that I was welcome to think it and not welcome to think I had done anything by thinking it.

And here is the part I was not prepared for.

I asked the Directory desk to pull Dhurin's status flag while I still had the terminal in front of me, mostly out of habit, the same habit that has had me checking a number at the bottom of every page in this case without quite admitting why.

Dhurin is not Concluded.

It is the only file in this entire survey that says so. Every world I have stood on, in this case and the one before it, carries the same closing flag eventually, mine included, mine especially. Dhurin's says OPEN, and it has said OPEN for four thousand clicks, and when I asked the Examiner why nobody at the Directory had ever come to close it out, she said nobody had, because the Kest keep asking them not to, formally, on schedule, through the correct channel, because of Exhibit Zero.

Exhibit Zero is a sealed container recovered near where one of the fourteen fell. It has never been opened. Kest law, their oldest procedural law, forbids it, and I assumed superstition and said so, and the Examiner corrected me without heat, the way she'd correct a first year.

It is not superstition. The generation after the deaths tried to open it. Someone's hand was on the seal. And somebody else in that room, watching, said: if this answers it, there is nothing left standing between us and what happened to them, and we have just spent a year watching what happens when nothing is left standing. So they wrote a law instead. Never open it. Keep the last question a question, on purpose, forever, by policy, reviewed and renewed every cycle since. Not because they don't want to know. Because they worked out, in one terrible year, exactly what happens to a world that runs out of things it doesn't know, and decided the not knowing was worth keeping.

I sat there for a while with that.

Because I have a wooden case, my dear readers. Ninety one slots. A rule I copied off a planet I visited once, that each page has to hold something nobody has ever written down, because I thought that was clever, because I thought I was the one who'd noticed that an account which never closes is an account that survives.

An entire civilisation got there before me. Four thousand clicks before me. And they didn't copy the shape of it off anybody's story. They built it out of a sealed box and a law and a refusal, renewed on schedule by people whose highest honour is knowing when to stop insisting they know something, and they have kept a whole world alive on it for longer than my own species has had a written language.

I told the Examiner, because the silence had gone on a beat too long and I didn't know what else to do with my face, that I'd come to Dhurin thinking I was going to write down the one thing nobody had ever put on a page, and instead I'd found the actual patent holders.

She said she didn't know what a patent was.

I said good, keep it that way, and left before I had to explain it properly.

This is Lu'kifær Morkinstar. Page nine of ninety one, in the case, not closed, waiting on the eighty two behind it.

**Terminologies:**

- **Kest** — Self-term of the Dhurinae. "The ones who have not closed it."
- **Examiner** — Ordained priesthood of Dhurin. Ordination is a defended cross examination, not a rite.
- **Clearing Hand** — Highest honour on Dhurin. Awarded for eliminating a suspect, never for proposing one. Given four times in four thousand clicks.
- **Exhibit Zero** — A sealed container from the night of the deaths. Never opened, by law, on purpose, renewed every review cycle.
- **Concluded** — Directory status flag. *Survey complete; no phenomena outstanding; no further contact indicated.* Six hundred and nineteen worlds.
