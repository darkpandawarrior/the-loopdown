# Every wrong number is in somebody's custody

Written after an audit fixed five real defects in a corpus that could have retconned all five as
unreliability, and after the finale was found carrying two errors in one paragraph where the design
called for one.

The anthology does not have an unreliable narrator. It has an honest narrator and an unreliable
record, and that is a stronger machine, but it is also a machine that will happily launder a typo.
This file is the guard on it.

## 1. The doctrine

**A wrong number ships only if you can name who is holding it. Name the custodian, or delete the error.**

Four custody classes. Pick one before you write the number, not after somebody finds it.

**Class 1. Held by the narrator.** He states the discrepancy himself, on the page, in his own
arithmetic. s2-06: six grams, then eight, then nineteen, and he does the subtraction out loud and
says he has stopped being able to call it instrument error. This class is not rationed. A narrator
doing sums in front of you is the entire basis of his credibility, and the more of these there are
the more the ledger reads as measured. The only constraint is that the gap must be a phenomenon and
not a slip: something an instrument could argue with.

**Class 2. Held by a named person in the world.** Somebody in the fiction knows it is wrong and is
protecting it. The milgalaxal: Ossul, twenty two galaxals, nine decks down, quietly failing to
action four correction requests, because the safest place for that number is a queue nobody reads.
This is the strongest class in the book. An error under a character's protection cannot be corrected
by anybody, in or out of the world, without deleting a person's decision.

**Class 3. Held by the form.** The institution's shape enforces it and the shape is visible on the
page. Field fourteen is printed grey, with a keyline, at exactly the same size as the other
thirteen, and revision forty three kept it white and waiting. Nobody shrank it and nobody reflowed
the section to thirteen. The absence has dimensions, so a reader can measure it. An error is legible
as an error when the container still shows the missing thing's size.

**Class 4. Held by the reader alone.** Nobody in the world knows, no line says so, and the reader
catches it against evidence the work has been showing since the first piece. **Rationed: at most one
per season, and it must be the season's ending.** Two of these in one frame is not two discoveries,
it is a typo and an alibi, because the reader cannot tell which one was the trap.

## 2. What this replaces

The rule proposed in session was *a deliberate error only reads as deliberate if it is the only
one*. That is true of class 4 and false of everything else, and shipping it as written would have
cost the corpus its best instrument. Four Directory errors stand uncorrected in Season One, the
milgalaxal stands across four hundred entries, and the case gains weight it cannot account for
through most of Season Two. None of that reads as sloppiness and none of it dims any of the rest.
Scarcity was never the variable. Custody is. The finale's two errors cancelled because both were
unheld and both sat in one paragraph, not because the corpus had run out of room.

**An unheld error is a typo.** That is the whole of the original rule that survives, and it is
enough.

## 3. How a reader tells, which is the fandom problem

A wiki will correct anything it can reach that looks like a mistake, forever, and a note saying
"this is intentional" is an author standing on a page whose claim is that there is no author. The
Speaker Test forbids it and the Speaker Test is right.

**The protection is diegetic or it does not exist.** The mechanism is already in the corpus, twice,
and it needs nothing new:

- #2300's terminology block, in Morkinstar's own voice, under the table: *The milgalaxal line does
  not multiply out. Do not correct it. It is the only place that world is still written down.*
- Ossul, who is the reason it was never corrected, and who is a person with a post and a chair.

An editor who deletes that number is deleting a stated in-world reason and a named man's twenty two
galaxals. That holds against a wiki in a way no production note ever will. So: **a class 2 or class
3 error must have its custodian on the page somewhere in the corpus, and once it does, the error is
finished and needs no other defence.** If you cannot get a custodian onto a page, you do not have a
protected error, you have a mistake you liked.

## 4. Where this file may go, and what it may never contain

The rule is craft. The register is a spoiler index. They do not live in the same room.

- **This file states the rule and the classes. It never lists which numbers are wrong.** A file that
  enumerates the protected errors is the same object as a kill record, and the leak doctrine already
  ruled on that object: `/making`, behind the declared-season spoiler gate, footer only, one way
  door. Same table, different room.
- **No class 4 error is named in any published doctrine, ever.** There is one per season and naming
  it is naming the ending. The working season bibles may state theirs, because a working bible is
  already a spoiler index and already barred from every fiction surface.
- **The doctrine may only be applied to errors already on the page. It may never license writing a
  new one.** The interloop bans retroaction-first design for the same reason and in the same words:
  found in shipped text, or it is a retcon and it is cut. A wrong number written to satisfy a
  doctrine is a puzzle. The milgalaxal is a headstone. The difference is that nobody meant the
  headstone.

## 5. The worked example, and it is the law's own counterexample

Law one says every world reports fourteen gods and fourteen monsters. The corpus's third entry says
of Sœbra: *She is the fifteenth god. Vædrun is the only world in Alpha Axmoiri with fifteen.* The
word "fifteenth" appears once in the whole anthology.

It is not a defect and it is not a protected error. It is the law's proof, and it was already
correct on the page.

#2259 gives the census in the past tense, of the legend: *many milgalaxals ago, in what the Sülm
call the Dry, the world of Vædrun was held between 14 gods and 14 monsters.* Sœbra was voted onto
the pantheon roughly nine hundred clicks after those events, by popular pressure, over the
priesthood's objection, on the argument that she was the only one who wrote it down. A census is a
count of what was there. A pantheon is a document. Documents get amended and this one was. Under the
two facings the fourteen are entities with a front and a back, the things Ilta could walk around;
Sœbra is a clerk who lost a scroll and has no back, and the entry never claims she is one of the
fourteen, only that she is on the list.

So the census is fourteen, the list is fifteen, and the Directory filed fourteen, because the form
has fourteen fields and there has never been a fifteenth. Morkinstar himself files Vædrun as his
third fourteen and fourteen and does not adjust his tally. The fifteenth exists only in prose, in
the second to last paragraph, in a sentence told to him by a woman gutting a fish on what had been
the sea floor two days earlier, and no form in the galaxy has anywhere to put it.

The one world that put its teller on the god list is the one world the record undercounts, by
exactly one, forever. That is the anthology's thesis executed in a single line in entry three,
before anybody knew what the anthology was about. Custody class 3, held by the form. Do not touch
#2259.

**The defect was in law one's phrasing, not in the corpus it governs**, which is the same shape as
the leak doctrine's finding: the rule was correct and was violated by the file it was written in.
Law one is restated in `bible.md` so that the next drafter cannot re-derive this as a bug and fix
the entry.

## 6. The check before you file a wrong number

1. Who is holding it. If the answer is nobody, it is a typo. Delete it.
2. If the answer is the reader, has this season already spent its one. If yes, one of the two is cut.
3. If the answer is a person or a form, is that person or that form on a page anywhere in the corpus.
4. Is the gap something an instrument could argue with, or is it just a slip.
5. Did you find it, or did you write it. If you wrote it, it does not qualify.
