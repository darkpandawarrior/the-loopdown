Season One was a man filing. Season Two was the same man refusing to. Season Three was that man burning ninety pages of his own work to stay alive. **Season Four is that man walking into a city that was built out of his filing, and posting on a wall.**

Broadcast, then never sent, then destroyed, and now executed.

## THE SPINE (this is the season, and nothing may contradict it)

**He tested the count on the gods and never on the bodies. The bodies were the checkbox. The checkbox got built.**

That is not a metaphor and no piece may treat it as one.

Fourteen worlds of Category 3 species who could not leave their planets. Then they could, and did, and what a galaxy of arrivals needs immediately is one shared way of describing a body to a body that cannot perceive it. Doorway width. Step riser. Handrail height. Seat pitch. Air mix. The height a sign goes on a wall so a person can read it. Every one of those is a number about a body, and at the merge there was exactly one dataset in the galaxy that held numbers about bodies: sixty galaxals of Galactic Directory survey forms, filed by field correspondents, through translation rigs, onto a form with a box on it that says *bipedal category species*.

Nobody conspired. A standards body did the responsible thing with the only evidence there was. The whole built galaxy is dimensioned off a checkbox, and the largest single contribution to the dataset it was dimensioned off is Corpus 2300, which is his.

So the city is a rendering, at civilisation scale, in concrete, permanent, and it is his handwriting.

**And the record has a price now, and nothing in this city is ever destroyed, only covered, and every quarter it is cheaper to carry the weight.**

Skerrin's mass is the record and it grows by exactly what you write. That is flat canon from #2284 and he transcribed it himself. Season Two was a man making the thing heavier one page at a time. Season Three was the same man making himself light again at the cost of two seasons of work and most of what he could still remember. Season Four is a jurisdiction that has done the arithmetic on both and concluded, correctly, that the fire is not worth the money.

There is a Deprecation Office. It is competent. There is a load figure on the Holding Tiers reinforcement notice, published quarterly because it is a safety notice. There is a costed proposal for a general unweighting and it has been on the deferred list a very long time, because the consultation alone exceeds the saving, and that is a correct decision, and it will be a correct decision forever. The Væhn spend a generation preparing for the fire. **The city priced that generation and declined it, politely, with reasons, in the public accounts.**

**What the city does instead of the fire is paint, and paint is not fire.** A cleared notice is covered, not burned. The layers stay on the wall, the ghosts stay under the primer, the fragments stay legible at the torn corners, and the load figure at the Holding Tiers is higher every quarter because nothing has ever actually left.

This governs the finale and it is not negotiable. When he reads the board in piece fourteen and works out that he has been getting the Kindling for free all season, **he is wrong, the season knows it, and he does not.** He has spent fourteen pieces posting pages into a wall that keeps every one of them, getting heavier while concluding he has finally become light, and the reader can check his arithmetic against every torn corner the plates have been showing them since piece one, and against Hevrit's held back panel, which is only possible on a wall that lets nothing go. **No piece states this.** The bottom corner lifts because the record does not let go of a page. He presses it back down. That is the sign off, and now it is the season.

**And the third thing, which is the engine, and which nothing may soften: the count falls because he is good at his job.**

In the first piece he writes an accurate, well argued, genuinely excellent notice about a fare gate that opens for nobody. It is read. The fault is fixed, correctly, by somebody competent, for the twelfth and last time. A file comes off the board. That is a real good and the season never sneers at it, and it is the beginning of the thing he spends fourteen pieces failing to notice.

The engine runs on the page more than once, and that is a requirement rather than an option. The correction Halkri tells him to file in piece eight closes the fourth riser file on the board in piece ten, so the reader watches his one read and referenced notice conclude the thing he loved, and Vessik's mechanism from piece six has a face on it.

No conspiracy. No secret. **No villain, ever, in any piece.** The cruelties in this season are all pricing decisions, each defensible in isolation, and the sum of them is a monster nobody is feeding on purpose and nobody can afford to starve. Every official is right, is good at their job, and can explain their reasoning in about six minutes without hesitating, which is Ossul's exact structure from #2300 reused deliberately.

**What this forbids, and it is not negotiable:** the season may never present a character who understands the problem and is thwarted by wickedness. The people who understand it are the ones who costed it. He is not smarter than they are. He carries one lesson that does not scale, learned personally at enormous expense, which is that a thing survives by periodically becoming light, and there is no version of that sentence that survives a procurement review, and he files it anyway, and it is acknowledged and not actioned. And the finale is that lesson misapplied by the one man who paid full price for it: **he mistakes being covered for being light, and nobody corrects him, because nobody in this city knows there is a difference.**

**Season One asked whether a record is true. Season Two asked whether a record keeps something open. Season Three asked what of you goes out with the page. Season Four asks what happens to a record when it stops being read and starts being executed.**

That line goes in the bible and nowhere else. No character says it, no aside gestures at it, no plate carries it.

## The two media, and they are the season

The author's objection to an earlier draft was correct and it was fatal to that draft: a man with paste and a sheet of paper is too small a medium for an age whose defining substance is information. The answer was already in the design and was sitting one level below where it belonged. It is now the spine.

**His medium is not the notice. His medium is the schema.**

The archive was bought as a dataset. It is the calibration source for the perception utility and it is the shape of every civic form in the merged jurisdiction. Doorway widths. Riser heights. Seat pitch. Handrail height. The medical baseline that decides whether a body reads as ill. The permitted values in a reach field. The greyed out fourteenth box. Every one of those is his first impression, taken once, through a rig, onto a form, never tested on a body, and it is **executing**, continuously, in concrete and in software, in eleven thousand jurisdictions, today, while he stands in a transit hall with a brush and a folded sheet.

So the two media are in tension and the tension is the whole season:

- **What he thinks his medium is:** a paper notice. Unlicensed, uncopiable, honest, posted by hand, cleared on a schedule. The last thing he owns.
- **What his medium actually is:** the schema. Unkillable, uncorrectable, load bearing, and already inside every building he walks through.

He is proud of the small one. He does not know he is holding the large one. **He never finds out**, and no piece may let him work it out, because the moment he understands the schema he becomes a man with a grievance, and a man with a grievance in a season with no villain is a man shouting at arithmetic.

The reader works it out somewhere around piece six, when Vessik disables field fourteen and the pause becomes a null rate on a form filed by everybody in the galaxy, and then spends eight pieces watching him post paper.

## The monster, detonated correctly

The previous draft carried Skerrin as background pressure. That is not enough, and canon supplies the detonation for free.

**Skerrin's mass is the record and it grows by exactly what you write.**

He has spent one Vænheim believing he controls the input. He does not, and he has not since the procurement committee signed. **Eleven thousand jurisdictions write on his form every day.** Every intake, every residency return, every accessibility certificate, every corrected self term, every stair certified against the code: all of it lands in the schema he wrote, and every one of them makes the thing heavier, at industrial scale, continuously, without him touching a stylus.

And he is trying to unweight himself nine minutes at a time with a sheet of paper.

That is the same defect as the free Kindling and it has the same fix, and the two are one thing: **he is doing subtraction on a system that is adding, and the addition is not his to stop, and the subtraction was never subtraction in the first place.** The season states neither half. It prints both in the small print, quarterly, as safety notices, where they belong.

One arithmetic guard, because a reader will check. **Writing on the schema is not a citation.** The citation meter counts lookups of Corpus 2300 as a source, which is what being read looks like. Eleven thousand jurisdictions executing his form generate exactly zero citations, because execution is not reading. That is why the two numbers in the footer move in opposite directions and why the one that would save him is the small one.

## How long has it been

One Vænheim.

Nobody says so, because nobody has to. The unit is in ordinary daily use: lease terms, heritage listings, the birth line on a form, the long column on a timetable. A unit named at the founding and left blank because nothing had yet lasted long enough to need it, given a length by a hand nobody identified on a page nobody dated at the end of Season Two, and now it is on a bus schedule.

He says one thing about the gap, in the first piece, in about two sentences: the click was rebased again at some point while he was not paying attention, the way it was rebased once before, and the conversion he would need to work out his own age is one of the lines that got restated, so the honest answer to how long is that the unit he counted in is not the unit any more. Then he never raises it again, because a man complaining about a conversion table is the oldest joke he has and he has decided to stop telling it.

**Vænheim is used and never explained. Elysheim still reads *not yet required*, and it appears exactly twice, and the second time is a piece.** Do not resolve either. Do not have anyone notice.

**And no piece states the gap in Earth years, or in any unit that would let a reader multiply it out.** Page 91 gave Vænheim a number and that number is enormous. Any casual "galaxals ago" in dialogue is fine as vagueness and fatal as arithmetic. If a piece needs a duration, use a lease term, a listing cycle or a resurface schedule.

## Where Morkinstar is, resolved

All three answers are true at once, and the frame is built so that each one makes the other two worse. Remove any of them and the other two stop working.

**1. He is still the correspondent.** Literally. He arrives, he asks, he names the teller, he writes it down, he posts it. The method is completely intact and completely obsolete, which is not the same as useless. It still works. It is simply that the thing it was built for, a world that cannot leave and therefore has one weather and one pantheon and one answer, does not exist any more, and the nearest surviving equivalent is a district.

He posts. He writes an entry, walks it to a municipal notice surface, pastes it up, presses the bottom corner down because the bottom corner is the one that lifts, and walks away **without keeping a copy.** That is the one thing he learned from the fire. It is not the right lesson, but it is a lesson.

**2. The archive became infrastructure, and this is the joke that hurts.** Corpus 2300, all two thousand three hundred entries, is the largest single body of verified self terms and body data anyone ever compiled, because he spent a career writing down what people call themselves and how far apart their hands are, rather than what a surveyor guessed. When the city needed a standard it bought the corpora that existed. **Four hundred entries of corrections**, acknowledged and not actioned, were eventually actioned, all of them, in bulk, galaxals after he stopped filing them, by a procurement committee, as a dataset purchase, and a per citation royalty goes to a rights administrator who has never heard of him. His disclaimer is the recorded line a fare gate plays when a rendering fails to resolve, because legal needed a form of words that promised nothing and his was already written and already licensed.

He stands in a city that is full of his voice, and the city cannot see him.

**The object that was sold is Corpus 2300, the broadcast entries. It is not the case.** The case was never sent, never copied, never licensed, and went into a fire, and every page in it was required by its own rule to contain something never written down anywhere, which makes it definitionally unduplicated. **No piece may imply a surviving copy of the case exists.** See the Interloop, where this is locked three ways and the temptation is refused by name.

**3. A council decides, by declining to decide.** The Deprecation Review meets four times across the season on the retention of Corpus 2300, because its citation rate is falling toward the threshold. It never reaches him. The item is deferred every time, not out of mercy and not out of malice, but because deleting a corpus of that size is itself a chargeable operation and the quarter it would land in always has something more urgent. Which is exactly what Ossul did with four correction requests for twenty two galaxals, except Ossul was a man protecting a number on purpose and this is a queue with nobody in it.

**And the fourth question, resolved and not hedged: he is a man, present, walking, with hands.** He is not a system, not a legacy, not a ghost, not a running process, and the season never once entertains that he might be. He is a man with no body who cannot be rendered, and that is a civic status with a form number, not a mystery. He is never described. He is never given a species. **He never gets a portrait, a poster, a face, or a frontage.** The only thing he is, is the account, and the account is being posted on a wall in a transit hall and painted over on a forty tick cycle, and also running, unattributed, in every building in the galaxy.

## The Rendering, in Season Four

This is the hardest constraint in the anthology and Season Four is the test it was built for. A far future where everyone finally shares one room does not falsify the doctrine that nobody has ever been seen. **It industrialises it, and it puts a meter on it.**

Unmediated perception between the species that used to be fourteen separate Category 3 worlds is not possible at street scale, and not for mystical reasons: they differ in size, resting temperature, pressure, tempo and sensorium, and several combinations are dangerous in both directions. So there is a utility. It is called **the Common**, it is a metered municipal service, it descends in a documented line from the translation rig, and it is legally defined as a representation rather than a view. This is stated once, flatly, in piece one, in the voice of a man reading a service leaflet, and it is never argued again.

Six consequences, all load bearing.

**One. Its calibration source is the Directory's own corpus.** It renders everyone very slightly toward the form, because that is what the training material says a person is. Nobody experiences this as deprivation. Rendering is what seeing is here, the way nobody experiences their own language as a filter. You can live an entire life in this city, raise children with somebody from another world, and never once see what any of them actually look like, and nobody is hiding anything.

**Two. Tiers.** Fidelity is a price point. A rich district resolves cleanly. A poor one bands and strains and resolves toward the familiar, which is exactly what a Directory instrument always did. That is not a metaphor for poverty, it is the actual budget line, and the plate carries the tier stamp so a reader can check.

**Three. He has no body, so there is nothing to render.** He does not appear in the civic layer at all. This costs him everything practical: he cannot pass a gate, hold a tenancy, or join a queue, because the queue is a rendering service. **And it makes him the only person in a city of metered mediated perception who has to be dealt with without the Common, which every teller in this season does.** On the Unrendered Mile nobody has the Common either, and that is exactly why he is not the exception there. This is a fact about this city and not about the anthology: the Rendering governs how a description reaches the reader, never whether two people in a room can deal with each other, and tellers have been putting things into his hands since Ilvra held out the syllabus. He is still never described, because he is the one holding the stylus.

**Four. It has never worked, and the season does not stage that, it prints it.** The service standard is published, because a metered utility has to publish one, and the line reads *representative, not exact*, with a stated tolerance and a complaints procedure. Nobody has ever been seen. The city knows. The city has a figure for it, a form for disputing it, and a tier at which the figure improves. That is the Ombri counting past eleven for four thousand clicks, at civic scale, converted into a service level agreement, and it is worse than the shed it replaced because nobody is even trying.

**Five. Being outside coverage is a class position and a service gap, and nothing else.** An unserved address is not a place where the truth comes through. It is a place where the utility is not provided, so the people there cannot perceive one another at all and manage the way people manage: sound through a floor, temperature at a doorway, a hand on a rail, a written note under a door, a bell you feel rather than hear. Nothing is revealed there. Nothing is forbidden there. The rents are good and there is a service charge.

**Six. He envies it, and not for the reason he would give you.** Once, quietly, on the Mile. He has never had a rendering to lose, and on an unserved street nobody has one either, so for nine days his condition is not remarkable and nobody has to be dealt with directly because everybody is. He is not seen. He is simply not the exception, for the only time in his life, and it is the closest thing to relief in the season, and it lasts nine days, because he cannot pay the service charge. **The unserved street has a service charge.**

## The Miéville fence, which is a rule and not a note

A blind seven lab ownership ensemble named **China Miéville's *The City & The City*** at frame level, repeatedly, independently. That is the real conviction in this season and it is not dismissible as pattern matching, because it landed on the frame rather than on the furniture.

**The fence, which is a genuine distinction and not a dodge:**

> That book is about people who **can** see each other and are **forbidden** to. It requires a taboo, a transgression, and a police force. This season is ten thousand species who **cannot** see each other and are **sold a utility**. There is no prohibition. No transgression is possible. The failure mode is not breach, it is a billing tier.

**The rule that follows, and it is not negotiable:**

**The season may never stage unmediated perception as a forbidden act, a revelation, or a transgression.**

Checkable consequences:

1. No character is ever punished, warned, policed or disciplined for perceiving anyone. There is no enforcement body, because there is nothing to enforce.
2. No place is a place where the truth is finally visible. There is nothing under the Common. The species genuinely cannot perceive one another; the utility is a prosthetic, not a veil. Switch it off and you get absence, not sight.
3. No scene may build toward contact and then deliver it, and no scene may build toward contact and then withhold it as tragedy. Both shapes are the same borrowed shape.
4. Nobody in the season wants unmediated perception. It is not desirable, not aspirational, not mourned. What people want is a better tier, a lower charge, or a working gate.

**This also repairs a series canon error the previous draft carried.** That draft had the Unrendered Mile as the one place in the anthology where people actually see each other, unmediated. **Nobody has ever been seen.** That is law, and Season Four is the test it was built for, not the exception that ends it. The fence and the canon agree, which is how you know the fence is real.

## The fourteen accessions, and the ones nobody surveyed

Fourteen worlds of Category 3 species arrived able to leave, and they founded the city, and they hold **accession numbers 01 to 14 in merge order**. The number is stamped on everything they touched: byelaws, licence classes, fittings, plant, permitted values in a field, the tolerance on a service standard. Nobody reads it, the way nobody reads a fire door certificate.

**Accessions did not stop at fourteen.** They run to a number the season never states. A plaque in the Returns Hall reads ACCESSION 31 and says nothing else. A licence class in the Lower Plenum is prefixed 47. There are worlds in this city that have left a valve, a byelaw and nothing else behind them, and there is no list anywhere in the season that would let a reader count them. **Any piece may put an unexplained accession number on an object. No piece may explain one.**

**Only two of the fourteen founders are worlds the reader has been to, and that ration is deliberate.** Fourteen districts descending from the fourteen published entries would make the season a curtain call, and it would make every district the same joke told with a different noun, which is the sameness the council flagged and which is the largest remaining risk in the design. Two callbacks is enough to detonate and not enough to close the loop.

**The rest of the variety comes back from physics, which is what Season One got for free and what this season has to buy.** A world nobody surveyed on the page has no published entry to contradict, so its bodies, its ground and its weather can be exactly as strange as the district needs them to be. The discipline is not on the strangeness, it is on the delivery. **A founder's homeworld never arrives as description. It arrives as the reason a thing in the city is shaped wrong**, in a mandated dimension, a fitting, a permitted value, a maintenance window, a byelaw with a number on it. This season's medium is the schema, so a world reaches the reader as a specification or it does not reach them at all.

**Rules on the roster, and every one of them is checkable.**

A number below 2245 means he wrote it early, and since the corpus runs #0001 to #2300 there are two thousand two hundred and forty four of those, and **he does not remember most of them.** The season may use that fact once. It may not use it twice.
2. **If a future season publishes one of these entries, it must satisfy the series position arithmetic in `bible.md`.** Within one system a series position rises by at most one per entry number. Run it before you assign a number, because it has already caught one real error in this anthology and it will catch the next one.
3. **No district descended from an off page world may explain itself in dialogue.** The teller may describe the fitting. The teller may not describe the homeworld. If a piece needs the homeworld, it goes in the amber strip as a citation, and the reader may go and look it up and fail to find it, and failing to find it is the correct outcome and the better one.

Do not let a returning reader be right for the wrong reason.
5. **Nothing in this roster may become a cosmology.** These are accession records. There is no founding myth of the city, no charter drama, no merge epic, no first day. The merge was an administrative event with a schedule, and it was boring, and the boredom is the joke.
6. **A founder that has left the city is not a tragedy and is not a mystery.** The Hœlmar are gone, the accommodation stands, and the season never says where they went, and no character wonders. Species move. The code does not.

## The iceberg

The author's second ruling, and it is the largest addition in this pass: there is to be **rich background a reader can go and find**, rather than background a narrator hands over. This season is the best carrier the anthology is ever going to get, because its medium is the schema and its device is environmental signage, and **a form, a plaque, a licence class or a byelaw can hold an entire world's history in six words to anybody who looks at it.**

Four depths, and **each one is complete at its own depth.** That is the rule every other rule here serves.

**Depth one: the piece.** A reader who reads only the prose gets the whole season and misses nothing they were promised. Every mechanism a piece turns on is stated in that piece, plainly, by somebody competent, in about six minutes without hesitating. **Nothing below this line is ever load bearing.** If a piece cannot be understood without depth two, the piece is broken and the fix is in the piece.

**Depth two: the plate.** The signage carries a second reading that the prose never gives. The district name, the byelaw number, the licence class, the coverage tier stamp, the resurface schedule, and the amber compliance strip at four points. A reader who reads the sign gets a joke the narrator did not make, which is exactly right for a season where his voice has been licensed out from under him. **The plate may differ from the prose in emphasis. It may never differ in fact.**

**Depth three: the codes.** Every calibrated object in this city carries the corpus entry it was calibrated from, because a metered utility and a merged building code both have to cite their sources. So the amber strip on plate three carries **#2269**, and a reader who goes and reads #2269 finds Ædri, and the ledger of cold holds, and the exact readings that became a species' medical normal. The strip on plate eight carries **#2277** and the reader finds the Hraedh, and a grandmother aiming stones to within a third of a degree, and then looks at the door mechanism again. **And the strip on plate two carries #0412, and there is nothing to find, because he filed that one before the reader ever met him.** Both outcomes are correct. The second one is better, and it is the whole season in one dead link: the city is dimensioned off entries nobody has read, and they are his, and he does not remember writing them either.

**Depth four: the register.** The site may publish the city's own documents and only the city's own documents. An accession schedule. A byelaw index. A licence class schedule. A form catalogue with permitted values. The resurface register. The published service standard for the Common, with its tolerance and its complaints procedure. **Never an essay, never a wiki, never a glossary, never annotations, never anybody explaining anything.** If a jurisdiction would not print it, it does not go in. A reader who wants the deep water gets a filing cabinet, and a filing cabinet is what this anthology has always been.

### The carriers

What can hold six words of a world's history, in descending order of how much it can carry without anybody noticing:

- **A permitted value list on a form field.** The strongest carrier in the season, because it is simultaneously the deep lore and the injury.
- **A licence class.** A letter and an accession number, stencilled under a speaker grille or on a bowl stall.
- **A byelaw number** on a sign, cited and never quoted.
- **A mandated dimension.** A riser height. A doorway width. A rail that does not stop.
- **A plaque** with an accession number and no name on it.
- **A tolerance** on a published standard, which tells you what the city has decided it can live with.
- **A calibration label.** Ondrit's tape set to a standard retired two rebasings ago. Ilvath's certified reference bottles.
- **A maintenance window** that does not line up with anything, because it was set by a body whose day is not this city's day.
- **A greyed out box**, which carries more than any of them and is already spent, once, correctly, in piece six.

### The worked example, and it is the model

The residency return has a field for sex with **two permitted values**.

The Marltains have more than two, and obligate twin birth, always. The Directory filed their binary as sex in **#2263**. Morkinstar found it, filed a correction, and it was acknowledged and not actioned. The field is his. It is on every form in the galaxy. It is executing right now.

**No piece says any of this.** What a piece may do is show a Marltain resident at a counter filling the form in, picking one, and moving on, because it is an ordinary afternoon and there is a queue. A reader who has read #2263 gets the entire history of an error, its author, its correction, its refusal, and its industrialisation, out of one person ticking a box. A reader who has not read #2263 sees somebody fill in a form. **Neither reader is missing anything they were promised, and only one of them is going to be quiet for a minute.**

That is the shape. Copy it.

### The seven rules that stop it becoming a puzzle box

The interloop already forbids the anthology from becoming one and this is the same defect one level down, so the rules are the same rules made specific.

1. **Nothing discoverable may be required.** Depth one is the season. Everything else is weather.
2. **No discoverable is ever plot.** The reward for digging is a fact about a world, never an answer to a question the season posed. Nothing down here resolves whether the fire worked, what the fourteenth is called, who the readers are, or what he looks like.
3. **No depth may contradict another.** A code that resolves to the wrong entry is worse than no code at all, and it is the failure mode that kills reader trust fastest, because the one reader who checks is the one reader you had.
4. **No character discovers the iceberg on the page.** He never does the digging, and nobody hands him a document that explains a district. The reader digs. He posts.
5. **Nothing in chrome may announce that there is depth.** No did you notice, no annotations, no commentary, no reading guide, no easter egg language anywhere. The city does not advertise its own filing system, and neither does this.
6. **Every discoverable is authored as a document, not as content.** Written in the register's voice, flat, correctly formatted, with a form number and a revision date, by somebody who was paid to write it.
7. **Numbers are stable across every piece, plate and document, and the register table in this bible is the authority.** Keep a codes ledger beside the bible while drafting: accession numbers, licence classes, byelaw numbers, cited entries, form numbers. One page, checked before each piece ships. The most expensive mistake available in this design is the cheapest one to prevent.

## The dread

Two numbers in the small print, and one word in everybody's mouth. Never remark on any of them more than once in the season.

### 1. Files Open

Season Two's dread was a Concluded count going up in a terminology block. Season Four inverts it and puts it on signage, which is the season's whole device. In a merged jurisdiction almost everything has been surveyed, so the interesting figure is not how many files are Concluded, it is how many are still **Open**, and that number is small enough to hold in your hand.

It is displayed on a departure board style fixture in a transit hall, in the city's own signage face, at a height and scale where several hundred thousand people walk under it a day, and nobody looks. It sits in the Notice conditions block at the foot of every piece as a bare line.

**19 · 19 · 18 · 18 · 17 · 15 · 15 · 14 · 12 · 11 · 12 · 9 · 5 · 3**

**And here is why it falls, which is the season.** A file closes when its last unresolved field is filled. Filling fields is how people get served: a body survey brings a household into coverage, a corrected self term makes a form work, an accurate notice about a broken gate gets the gate fixed. So the board falls because good people are doing good work, correctly, with his form, and every drop is somebody being helped and one fewer place where anything is still outstanding.

**And here is why it is not a countdown, which matters more than it looks.** A file opens the moment somebody writes something down, and it closes only once somebody has done the work. Opening is a clerical act and takes an afternoon. Closing takes a maintenance cycle, a certification, and eleven thousand jurisdictions getting round to it. So the board rises immediately and falls slowly, and **it rises in piece eleven**, on the page, in the footer, from 11 to 12, because his accreditation turns out to be open and an open survey is a file. That is the only rise in the season, it is his, and no line anywhere remarks on it. **Three is not a floor, no schedule takes it to zero, and it can be back in double figures inside a quarter if anybody reports anything the city has no field for.**

The board is citywide, so most movements are other people doing the same job at volume, and **four of them are his and those are the ones that hurt.** The gate from piece one, posted in one and landing in **five**, because a fault has to be fixed and then certified before the last field is filled, and by then he is three districts away. Field fourteen, disabled in six and landing across **eight and nine** in two tranches, because a form change has to be processed by every jurisdiction that uses the form and they do not all do it in the same period. The fourth riser, filed in eight on Halkri's instruction and landing in **ten**, while he is standing on the Reconciliation Floor. And his own accreditation, opened in **eleven** and landing in eleven, because openings do not wait. **The drop at six is volume and not Vessik**, and a drafter who forgets that will make the arithmetic stop closing. The reader should work the mechanism out somewhere around piece six and spend the back half of the season watching kindness close files.

Vessik states the mechanism once, in piece six, in about six minutes without hesitating, and she is right.

Two files never come off it. **Dhurin**, which has said OPEN since the night the Kest say everything that had ever been a god or a monster on their world died, which is their account and not a settled finding, and which stays open because the Kest keep formally asking, on schedule, through the correct channel, and in a merged jurisdiction that request is a quarterly file maintenance fee, and they have paid it every quarter without missing one. That is the exact current price of a refusal that was already four thousand clicks old when he last stood on Dhurin and is one Vænheim older now: a standing charge. And there is a numeric designation nobody will read out. And, from piece eleven onward, **his own accreditation**.

What happens at zero is answered in piece fourteen and it is not apocalyptic, it is worse: the fixture stays, the power stays, and the board becomes an advertising surface, because an empty display is a wasted asset. **And the contract for that is already signed.** It was executed a long time ago, it sits in the published register like everything else, and it does not carry a commencement date, it carries a commencement condition, and the condition is zero. Nobody is waiting for it. It is simply correctly filed, against a number that can go up.

### 2. The citation meter

In the Notice conditions block, under the licence line, one figure: citations of Corpus 2300 this momenta, against a retention threshold of 400.

**1,206 · 1,140 · 998 · 941 · 907 · 803 · 1,004 · 744 · 690 · 651 · 588 · 502 · 461 · 397**

The jump at seven is caused by six: Vessik's disablement notice cites Corpus 2300 as the source of the field, every jurisdiction that reads the notice looks the citation up, and **an act of deletion keeps him alive for one accounting period.** The last figure is below the line, on the last page, with nothing happening, because the review item is deferred.

**And below the line is a state, not an event, which is the entire difference between an ending and a season.** The retention rule is published, because everything here is: an item becomes actionable after **four consecutive momenta** below the threshold, and one momenta at or above it resets the count to zero. So the footer carries a third figure, `momenta below threshold`, which reads 0 for thirteen pieces and **1** on the last page, and no piece remarks on it, and a reader who has been reading the small print since piece one watches a field change value for the first time in the season on the page where he decides he is light. What that leaves is exact, and survivable, and it has teeth: the clock has started, it did not exist before, it can only be stopped by people looking him up, and looking him up is the one thing he has never been able to make anybody do.

The cruelty is the exact inversion of Season Two's spine. There, every page he wrote made the monster heavier. Here, the only thing keeping his account from deletion is **being read**, and the people reading him are not scholars, they are ordinary residents looking up what their own grandparents called themselves because the Common has it wrong and his file is the only place it is right. He is kept alive by about forty people a quarter checking a self term. Every one of those lookups is a mark on the record and the record is a body.

And the eleven thousand jurisdictions running his schema every day count for nothing here, because they are not reading him. That number never appears in the footer. It has no field.

### 3. The word

In this city **cleared** means painted over. It is in the header of every piece. It also means a file closed, and a fault fixed, and a district finished, and by piece four you will hear somebody use it about a person who died.

Nobody in the season explains it. Nobody remarks on it. It never appears in a Terminologies block because there is no Terminologies block. A reader who has been here since #2300 knows exactly which Directory word it is standing in for and the season never says that either.

**And one more register that is already in canon and must stay untouched:** on Dhurin the highest honour since the night they count from is **Clearing Hand**, awarded for eliminating a suspect and never for proposing one. Nobody in Season Four connects the two uses. Do not let them.

## The Interloop

Ruled this session by the coherence custodian, and it governs how Season Four sits against the other three. It is never advertised, never explained, never performed, and no character or page of chrome names it.

### The thesis

**The record is never finished being read.** Each season is a rendering of the seasons before it, and a rendering can hold, strain, fail, or refuse. When a new season lands, what an earlier one meant moves, and what it cost does not. **A later season may reprice a page. It may never refund one.** The seasons therefore work separately, together, or in any order, because an account with an unwritten page in it is not complete, and a thing that is not complete cannot be Concluded.

### The medium loop, and it closes

The four media are four relations between the record and its reader.

| Season | Medium | The relation |
|---|---|---|
| One | broadcast | read by everyone |
| Two | never sent | read by no one he can name, and then canon reveals the case read itself |
| Three | destroyed | unreadable |
| Four | executed | run without being read at all |

**Execution is the return leg, because a schema is a broadcast with the reader removed.** It reaches everyone, constantly, forever, with the authorship stripped. So Season Four is Season One at industrial scale, and that is the horror.

Canon already supplies the proof of medium. A record that is read can be argued with, and his corrections were **acknowledged and not actioned**, which is what being read looks like. In Season Four the corrections are actioned in bulk as a dataset purchase, which is not an argument, it is an ingestion. And the fare gate plays his disclaimer as a recorded courtesy, which is a sentence being executed rather than heard.

The loop is closed causally as well as thematically: the broadcast could never be burned, and the fire proved it by only being able to touch the never sent case, so the broadcast aged for one Vænheim and became the thing that gets executed.

And the media interlink inside Season Four, which is the interloop's strongest form. **The wall is the case at civic scale**: accumulation, nothing destroyed, only covered. **The city is the broadcast poured in concrete.** **The fire exists as a costed proposal, priced and declined in the public accounts.** Season Four does not follow the three prior media. It contains them as infrastructure.

**One condition, non negotiable.** The closure is only real under the revision at the head of this bible. If piece fourteen's free Kindling stands as fact, paint equals fire, Season Four contains a working Season Three at zero cost, the media collapse into one another, and the loop is not a loop, it is a drain.

## Season Four is not the last season

Ruled by the author, and it changes what the season's instruments are for. ### Why this is not a commercial accommodation, which matters

The anthology already forbids itself an ending and does it in canon, twice. **#2300 is filed incomplete** and ends mid sentence, because a record that is not finished cannot be Concluded, and *"anyone who fixes that sentence has deleted the ending."* **The Væhn keep one blank page** for the same reason, and Season Three ends with him doing it with an object instead of a typographic mark. The interloop's own thesis is that **the record is never finished being read.**

This is the part that is not allowed to soften, because a season that resolves nothing is not the answer either and a survivable ending is one draft from a weightless one.

## Art doctrine

**An S4 plate is a fly posted municipal notice on a city wall, photographed at night under the district's own lighting.**

Season One is the Directory's dark survey form, an institution documenting immaculately what it did not understand. Season Two is his own warm paper page, hand measured. Season Three is that page scorched, foxed, curled and gone. Season Four is what happens to a page in a city: it gets pasted to something, and the something already has forty other pages under it. The four seasons must be identifiable as objects from across a room, and this one is identifiable because it is the only one that is wet, lit from outside, and stuck to a surface that is not his.

### The chrome, byte identical on all fourteen, only the middle changes

**The surface.** A public notice panel with the ghosts of previous notices under the paint, layers visible at the torn corners and along the bottom edge where the paste has failed. This is the damage register and it is the exact inverse of Season Three's: that was destruction, **this is accumulation**. A plate carrying a legible fragment of an earlier S1 or S2 plate under the current paint is the payoff device, used sparingly, and the fragment is always from the entry the piece detonates.

**And since the revision, the torn corner is evidence and not texture.** It is the reader's audit trail against the finale, so it is drawn to a rule rather than to taste:

- Every plate shows countable layers at at least one torn corner, from plate one.
- The count rises across the season, and the wall is measurably thicker at plate fourteen than at plate one, at the same crop and the same scale.
- No plate ever shows a stripped panel, a bare substrate or a wall being taken back to anything. There is no such operation in this city.
- Plate fourteen's corner is the thickest object in the season, and it is behind him while he works out that he is light.

**The district signage plate**, top of frame, dominant, in the city's own signage face, large, correctly kerned, professionally installed, and **it is over his notice**, because the signage layer has right of way and a fly poster does not. Every plate is therefore a city sign with a man's handwriting underneath it, which is the season in one object.

**And the signage layer carries the district's accession number**, on a plaque, at signage scale, unexplained, every plate. Districts with no lineage carry a civic code in the same slot instead, which is how a reader learns that some places in this city came from somewhere and some places are just the city. A plate for an off page founder's district may show a fitting whose shape only makes sense for a body the reader has never seen: a rail that does not stop, a riser cut for a gait, a valve sized for a hand nobody in the frame has. **It may never show that body.**

**The permit strip** along the bottom. Where Season One had corner ticks and Season Two had a case and slot frame, Season Four has a municipal licence bar: surface owner, resurface schedule, compliance mark, stencilled licence code.

### The pictogram, which is the doctrine

The illustration inside the notice is drawn as **wayfinding**, because a pictogram is a rendering. The universal figure on every accessibility notice in every building in the galaxy is *bipedal category species*, drawn once, flattened, copied forever, and this anthology has spent thirty four entries arguing that is exactly what happened to everyone in it.

So the body in the middle of the plate is **flat schematic vector: no interior, no face, no eyes.** And **the hands are ink and crosshatch at a fidelity nothing else in the frame has, and they are busy.** Ondrit's tape. Sævrin's wheel. Ædrin's log. Ilvath's reference bottle. Osvær's thumb on the pillar mark. Guldri's lamp. Hevrit's roller.

This is exactly backwards from Seasons One to Three, where the rig resolved the person and strained at the cosmic, and that is the point: the city renders every body into the icon and the one thing it can never flatten is what somebody is doing with their hands. The sixty three hands tether from `species.md` is not relaxed anywhere in this season. It is the structure.

**And his plate.** He has no body and never gets one. In a city that renders everybody that resolves into the best image the anthology has ever had access to: **a person shaped gap in the civic layer, visible only as the place where the signage does not tile**, where the projected wayfinding runs across the wall and does not run across him. He is drawn as an interruption in someone else's graphics. Nothing else.

## Voice contract

His, unchanged in kind, and that is the asset: the S3 audit ruled that readers have spent thirty four entries becoming co-conspirators in his cadence and that his voice was never the fatigue.
