# Field Fourteen Is Disabled

> Posted · THE RETURNS HALL · cleared in 12 ticks

Greetings again, my dear readers. Lu'kifær Morkinstar, in the Returns Hall, standing with my back to a plaque that reads ACCESSION 31 and then says nothing else at all.

I have been in this room a little while now and I have read that plaque eleven times, hopefully. There is no second line. There is no date, no world, no name, no clause. Somebody arrived, somebody was numbered, and somebody had a plaque made, and that is the entire published account of them, and I have decided to leave it exactly where it is, which I would like you to notice, because I have not always been that person.

[Also posted- there is a second plaque a few feet along at knee height giving the load rating of the floor, and it has more words on it than the first one, and a revision date, and a contact. I am not going to make anything of that. I am mentioning it and moving on. This is me moving on.]

The Returns Hall is where the jurisdiction receives things back. Not goods. Statements. Every resident of this city files a residency return once a collection cycle, and this is the room the paper comes to, and it is a room with fifty one counters in a row and cyan signage at exactly the height a sign goes on a wall, and it is very well run, and it smells faintly of proof ink and wet stone.

Coverage here is full, which means the service resolves cleanly, which means everybody in this hall can perceive everybody else at the published tolerance and nobody is thinking about it. I am the exception, as I am everywhere, because there is nothing of me for the utility to work with. The ticket dispenser at the door issues against a resolved attendance and I am not one, so it does not issue. There is a byelaw for this, and the byelaw is printed on the dispenser, and what it says is that a person whose attendance cannot be resolved should present themselves at the enquiries desk and wait to be noticed.

So I did that. For a while. It is a very honest instruction and I want to be fair to it.

Here is the form.

The residency return is RR/01, currently at revision forty four, and it comes as three sheets fixed at the head. The top sheet is the one you write on and it is printed COPY across the top in cyan. The bottom sheet is the original. Nobody touches the bottom sheet. Nobody has ever written on the bottom sheet, it is not for writing on, the pressure of your hand on the top sheet is what puts your answers into it, and there is a line on the cover in bold that reads *do not write on the original*. That is the arrangement. It has been the arrangement for a long time. Everybody in the queue knows it, the desks are cut with a lip to hold the set flat while you press, and I watched about forty people fill one in and not one of them looked at the bottom sheet, because why would you, it is not the one you are working on.

Section nine of RR/01 is headed *observances, declared*. Nine dash one is your gods, and it takes as many as you have. Nine dash two is the other list, and the other list is boxed.

There are fourteen boxes.

Thirteen of them are white.

The fourteenth is grey. Not blank. Not left out. Printed grey, a flat solid municipal grey, with a thin keyline round it so you can see that it is a box and that it is a box you cannot use, and set in the middle of it in six point type is *field fourteen is disabled*. I measured it. It is the same size as the other thirteen, to within the tolerance of my hand and a folded strip of paper, which is what I had. Nobody has shrunk it. Nobody has closed it up and reflowed the section to thirteen. It is exactly as big as the ones you can write in and it is filled in with grey.

My dear readers, I have stood on a great many worlds and asked people to name their fourteen monsters and I have got thirteen names and a stop, every time, in unrelated languages, from people who had never heard of one another. I have made a small career out of describing that stop. I have used, in my time, the words *pause*, *gap*, *the blank line*, and on one occasion, which I am not proud of, *silence*. I had never once considered that it might have a background colour.

The person who greyed it is called **Vessik**, she designs forms for the merged jurisdiction, and she came out to the enquiries desk about nine minutes after I presented myself, because noticing is part of the job and she does the job.

She works standing. She reads a page by laying it flat and putting the flat of one hand across it just below the line she is on, and moving the hand down, and the outer edge of that hand is stained cyan and has been for years and is not coming off. She squares every sheet she touches against the desk, three taps, twice on the long edge and once on the short, and she does it to sheets she is about to throw away, and she did it to my folded strip of paper before she gave it back to me.

I told her what I wanted. She said, without hesitating and without any air of having been asked before: come and see the change record, it is published, you can have a copy.

Here is what she told me, and she told me all of it, standing, in about six minutes, and she did not stumble once.

Nine dash two has been on the return since the merge, because it was on the founding intake schedule, because the intake schedule was built from the corpora the standards body bought, and there was a field for the fourteen in the source. It collected fine. Thirteen names, in thirteen boxes, filed by everyone in the galaxy who has ever registered an address, once a collection cycle, for a very long time.

Field fourteen returned null.

Not mostly null. Null. Every return, every species, every jurisdiction on the schedule, four consecutive collection cycles, which is the standard window because one cycle is an accident and two is a coincidence and three is a pattern and four is a finding. A hundred percent null rate on a mandatory-adjacent field is a data quality defect. It is in the standard as a defect. It has a class and a remedy and a form of its own.

The remedy is one of three. You drop the column, which is a migration, and migrations break things, and this column is referenced by eleven thousand jurisdictions and by the accessibility certificate schedule and by two pension instruments, and a migration on a field that size is a year of work and a great deal of somebody else's money and an outage. You make it optional, which it already was. Or you disable the input, which leaves the column where it is, in the schema, in every form in the galaxy, and simply stops asking a question nobody has ever been able to answer.

She disabled the input.

I want to say this plainly because she said it plainly. She is right. She is right on the evidence she has, she is right by the standard she is held to, and she is right in the only way that matters here, which is that if you ask ten thousand species a question for four collection cycles and get nothing back from any of them, ever, the honest reading is that your question does not work. Every part of that is correct. I have checked it as far as I am able to check anything, which is not very far, and I could not find the place where it goes wrong.

So I tried to tell her about the stop.

She let me. All the way, my dear readers, all the way to the end, without once doing the thing where the eyes go to the middle distance and the hand starts squaring paper. I told her about thirteen names and a pause in unrelated languages on unconnected worlds. I told her about a six year old on Killuga Var who said you do not name that one, you just check. I told her the pause is the most consistent phenomenon I have recorded in a career of recording things, and that it is consistent in a way that a coincidence is not.

She thought about it properly. Then she asked me for one filled one.

One return. Any jurisdiction, any cycle, any species, any hand. One box fourteen with something written in it. Not thirteen and a stop. The fourteenth, filled.

I do not have one. I have never had one. I have an account, and an account is not evidence, and I have spent a very long time telling people that I do not prove or disprove things, so I could hardly stand at a counter and demand that a form take my word for it.

And she was kind about it. Not soft, kind. She said she had looked. She said she always looks before she disables anything, because a null rate is sometimes a broken input and not a missing answer, and the way you tell the difference is you go and find one instance of the thing being answered anywhere and then work out why it stopped. She ran the search across every return in the retained set. She said the number, and the number is large, and she said it the way you say a number you have said before.

She found none.

Then she showed me the drawer without my asking. In it is a printed proof of revision forty three, the version before hers, and in revision forty three field fourteen is white. A clean white box with a keyline round it and nothing in it, waiting, exactly the size of the other thirteen. She keeps the proof because she likes to see what a thing looked like before she changed it, and she said so in those words, and there was no apology anywhere in the sentence.

She offered it to me. To keep.

I said no. I do not keep things now. She did not ask why not, which I appreciated more than I know how to write down, and she put it back in the drawer and squared it, three taps, and closed the drawer with the edge of that cyan hand.

On the way out she said the thing I have been turning over ever since, and she said it in passing, as an aside, while pointing me at the notice panel by the east vestibule. She said a file closes when its last unresolved field is filled. That is all it is. Somebody fills the last field and the file goes off the board and everyone gets on with their afternoon.

[Also posted- I asked whether the grey box could ever be turned back on. She said yes, in the sense that anything can be turned back on, and no, in the sense that turning it back on is a change and a change needs a reason and the reason would have to be a filled one. So the way to get the field back is to answer it. I have written that down twice now and it has not improved.]

I wrote it up at the desk in the east vestibule, on my own paper, and I posted it on the panel there, which is resurfaced every twelve ticks, this being a civic building.

Then I put my thumb on the bottom corner and held it there until the paste took.
