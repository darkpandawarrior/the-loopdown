# What You Have Not Said Out Loud

> Page 23 of 91

Greetings again, my dear readers. Page twenty three of the ninety one, and I want to tell you up front that this is the page where I did not do the thing I do. You will see what I mean by the end of it, or you will not, and either way I am going to write around the middle of this one rather than through it, which I am aware is a strange thing to admit on the exact same page where I am about to describe a civilisation that has made not doing that more or less illegal, in every sense except the legal one.

Threnn is a wet green world orbiting a low orange sun in a system I never did get a straight answer about, weather that never quite commits to a season, moss on everything that holds still long enough. Its people file, according to the Directory, as **Thrennid**, Category 3, assessment on record reading *chronic disclosure behaviour, mild societal instability risk*, which I am going to come back to because it is wrong in a way I found genuinely irritating. Their own word for themselves is **Ovai**. The rig gives it to me flat and immediate, no hedging, the way it almost never does. It renders as "the spoken."

I want to sit on that translation for a second, because most self terms I collect are aspirational, or apologetic, or both. The counted. Those who wait. Those who begin. The Ovai are none of that. They are simply, factually, describing what they do all day, which is talk.

Here is the rule, and it is the whole of it, and I have never had a rule fit on one line before.

A thing can only harm you if you have not said it out loud.

That is it. That is the entire cosmology of Threnn, as far as I could get anyone to state it formally, and every Ovai I met treated it the way you or I would treat the fact that fire is hot. Not a belief. A property of the world, observed, confirmed, planned around.

What it means in practice took me most of a tick to understand and I am still not sure I have the mechanism right, so take the following as a correspondent's best attempt and not a physicist's. Every Ovai, privately, individually, grows something the rig renders as **a Kept**. It is not one monster shared by a pantheon the way I have been filing them for twenty two pages now. There is no single named thing chasing this whole species the way Uhl chases Grïnjdarlay or Skerrin sits on every mark the Væhn have ever made. A Kept belongs to exactly one person and is shaped, exactly, precisely, out of whatever that person has not said. Not a metaphor for it. Not a symbol standing in for it. The unsaid thing itself, given enough time and enough silence, apparently develops edges.

A small unsaid thing grows a small Kept. Most Ovai carry a dozen of them at any given moment and nobody thinks anything of it, the way you would not think anything of a person clearing their throat. Only the large ones concern them, and a Kept only gets large one way, by staying unsaid a long time in the presence of people who could have been told.

So they tell. Constantly. Immediately. Out loud, to whoever happens to be standing nearest, as a matter of what I can only describe as civic hygiene.

I watched a woman at a fruit stall hand a boy his change and tell him, in the same breath, completely unprompted, that she had been short with his mother the week before over a debt that was not actually the mother's fault, and that she intended to say so to the mother directly by evening, and that she was telling him now because he was there and she did not want it sitting in her a moment longer than it had to. The boy nodded like she had told him the price of the fruit. He was twelve, maybe. This did not strike him as an unusual thing to hear.

[Page Fact- they apparently do this to livestock too. I watched a farmer near what the rig kept calling Second Bank spend eleven straight minutes explaining to a heifer exactly why he had resented it since spring, in full sentences, with specifics. The heifer did not appear moved either way. I do not know what I expected]

I did measure it, because measuring is the one thing I am reliably good for and because a rule this tidy deserves to be checked by somebody who is not charmed by it. Over four mornings in the same market I paced the standing distance between people in conversation: two hundred and eleven pairs, and the median came out at fourteen inches, which is closer than any species I have filed and about half what the Marltains will tolerate. Eleven pairs stood further apart than thirty inches. All eleven, when I asked, turned out to involve somebody who had something they had not got round to saying yet, and every one of them named it to me without being asked twice. It is checkable, my dear readers. That is the part I want on the record. I paced it myself and the numbers hold.

I did not pace the distance around me. I want that on the record too.

I want to be honest about how it felt to be around, because the honesty is sort of the whole point of the place and I would be a hypocrite to file this entry any other way. It is exhausting. Two days in I found myself flinching slightly whenever anyone made eye contact, because eye contact on Threnn is very often the opening move of somebody about to tell you something true and specific about themselves that you did not ask for and cannot politely decline. Grief, debt, old resentment, who they wanted and did not get, what they thought of your coat. All of it, immediately, as if withholding were the rude thing and disclosure were the courtesy, which on Threnn it is.

And I am going to say the uncomfortable part now rather than let it sit unsaid on a planet that would find that extremely funny. It is also, near enough, the most emotionally healthy civilisation I have filed in this entire run. Nobody I met was carrying anything heavier than a few days old. Nobody flinched from a hard conversation, because hard conversations on Threnn happen at the size of a hangnail, constantly, before they can become anything else. I have watched a civilisation four thousand clicks along spend a generation at a time forgetting on purpose. The Ovai have apparently solved the same problem by simply never letting it accumulate, and I found I could barely stand two ticks of it, and I have had to ask myself, more than once since, what exactly that says about a man whose entire profession is other people's stories.

It put me in mind, more than once, of a storyteller on Solvei, several entries from here, who told me that a thing seen from behind is not a different thing from the front of it, only a different facing of the same one. I am not going to build a theory out of the echo. I have had that argument with myself before and lost it decisively, on the record, in front of you. But it is hard not to notice the Ovai have simply moved the whole business to human scale. What you have not said is not a separate problem from what you have said. It is the back of it. Same thing, standing where nobody has walked around to look yet.

I was assigned, as I usually am on Category 3 worlds that have any interest in speaking to a Directory correspondent at all, a person to show me around. Her name, as close as the rig will sit still for it, is **Ÿssa**. She is a teacher of small children, which on Threnn apparently means she spends most of her professional life helping six year olds learn to say things out loud badly rather than not say them well, and she was patient with me in a way I recognised, eventually, as the exact same patience she uses on the six year olds.

On the second day she stopped mid sentence, looked at me for slightly longer than felt comfortable, and said, gently, the way you would point out that somebody had a thread caught on their sleeve, that I was carrying something.

I told her I did not know what she meant.

She said that was, itself, informative.

I want to be precise about what happened next because I have turned it over a great many times since and I do not think she was performing anything. She said Ovai can tell, mostly without trying, the way you or I can tell when a room has gone quiet in a particular direction. Not the content of it. Never the content, she was careful to say that twice. Only the shape of a person who has one. A kind of hitch, she called it. A place where a person does not quite finish turning toward you.

She offered to listen. Not urgently, not as an intervention, the way you would offer someone a seat. I said I was fine. She let it go for the rest of that day, which I took, wrongly, as the end of it.

She raised it again on the fourth day, over a meal I do not remember the name of, and this time she told me the story, because I think she had decided that I needed the mechanism explained rather than simply named.

Many milgalaxals ago, she said, in the age before the rule was a rule, there was a woman named **Sevra**, who kept something. The particulars vary by teller and Ÿssa would not commit to which version was true, which I respected. In most versions it is a guilt. She had been present at a death she believed, wrongly or rightly nobody can say now, she could have prevented, and she did not say so, not to the dead woman's family, not to anyone, for reasons that made complete sense to her at the time and stop making sense to everyone the moment they are said out loud, which is rather the whole thesis of the planet.

Her Kept grew.

It did not chase her, Ÿssa was firm about that. It did not need to. It simply stood nearer to her than anything else did, a little closer every season, made exactly and only of the shape of the thing she had not said, visible to everyone but her the way your own back is visible to everyone but you. People began to angle around her at gatherings the way you angle around a doorway somebody has left a chair blocking. Nobody was cruel to her. They simply, gradually, stopped standing close, because there was less and less room to.

She spoke it at last at her own mother's funeral, apparently by accident, the way the truest confessions on Threnn tend to arrive, mid sentence, aimed at nobody, overheard by everybody. Ÿssa says the Kept did not vanish. It thinned. It stands, in every telling, somewhere just behind Sevra's account of her own life, small now, harmless now, the way an old scar is still technically a wound.

That is the whole of the rule, dressed as a woman instead of stated as a law. Ÿssa finished the story and looked at me the way she had looked at me two days before and said that whatever mine was, she would sit with me for as long as it took, and that nobody on Threnn would think less of me for however badly I said it, because everybody says the first one badly, that is rather the point of a first one.

I sat with that for a long moment, my dear readers, at a table on a world where honesty is load bearing infrastructure, and I thought about a page in a wooden case on a planet several systems from here, and a single word on it, in a child's hand, that I read once, closed the case on, and have not written down since.

I did not tell her.

I want to be exact about why, since precision is the entire courtesy I owe a place like this. It was not privacy. Ÿssa had made it very clear privacy was not a value anybody on Threnn organised their life around. It was not fear of judgment, she had already told me there would not be any. I think, if I am honest, and I am trying very hard this page to be, it was that saying it would have made it real in a way that not saying it, however badly that is going for me, still lets me pretend is optional.

She asked once more, at the transit dock, quietly, not in front of anyone, which I noticed and was grateful for even as I said no again.

The shuttle lifted off Threnn a little after local dusk and the planet went small in the window the ordinary way planets do. Nobody on the crew deck asked me anything, because crew decks never do, and I was glad of that too, and I am aware of exactly how that sounds coming directly after everything I have just told you about a world I claimed to admire.

I still have not said it. Not to Ÿssa, not to this page, not to anyone.

It came with me.

Is there truth in their legend? No one knows. My aim is never to prove or disprove the tales I come across. Just to report them to you, my dear readers, which today feels like a very strange thing to be proud of.

This is Lu'kifær Morkinstar. Page twenty three of ninety one.
