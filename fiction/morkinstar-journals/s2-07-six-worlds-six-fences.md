# Six Worlds, Six Fences

> Page 38 of 91

Greetings again, my dear readers. Page thirty eight, and it is time I told you the rest of a story I already told you the ending to.

You may remember Entry #2277, if you have been with me a long time, or if you have simply been paying better attention than I currently am to my own back catalogue. Jötunheimr. The Hraedh bury their dead standing up, four billion of them and still rising, in a line eleven thousand measures long, and every single grave is surveyed individually, at the hour of the burial, to face the same fixed point in the sky. Not a direction. A bearing. A number with a decimal in it, checked against the stars at the moment the stone goes down, because a planet turns and a fixed point does not hold itself still for you.

I asked what they were pointing at. The senior mason told me they were not pointing at anything. They were pointing the way it went. Skalde, their fourteenth, their cold that walks in on the dark side and takes what it finds standing still, was never killed in that legend. It withdrew. And a people who have spent four thousand clicks learning that a monster which leaves in a direction is a monster with a direction to come back from will aim every single thing they bury at that direction, forever, on the theory that a fence you stop maintaining is not a fence.

I made you a promise in that entry. I said I would keep taking the same measurement everywhere I went afterward, on every world with anything aimed at anything, and that I would report the result to you either way.

I kept it. Entry #2300. I had Ossul pull the files. Six worlds on record with a bearing in them. Six numbers. I compared them to each other the way you would compare six compass headings, and they did not agree, not even loosely, and I told you so, and I was relieved, my dear readers, genuinely relieved, to be allowed to let a thing go for once instead of carrying it into the next entry.

I should not have been relieved. I had done the comparison correctly and it was still the wrong comparison, and it took me eleven days sitting in a room nine decks down with nothing else to do before I worked out why.

Here is the mistake, and I want to walk you through it slowly, because I made it in public and I would like the correction to also be in public.

A bearing is not a direction. A bearing is a direction from somewhere. Two people standing in two different rooms of the same house can both be pointing at the exact same window and, if you only write down the compass heading each of them is pointing along and never write down which room they were standing in, you will conclude their two arrows have nothing to do with each other. That is what I did. I took six numbers off six survey forms and set them next to each other as though they shared an origin, because that is how you compare directions, and it is the correct first thing to try, and it told me nothing, because none of those six worlds shares an origin with any other. They are not in the same room. They are each in their own room, a very long way from the others, and I never once asked Deep Registry where the rooms were.

Ossul asked me if I wanted the coordinates. He has them. Deep Registry keeps the position on file for every world the Directory has ever surveyed, because that is a filing requirement, and filing requirements are the one thing this organisation has never once let slip.

[Page Fact- Deep Registry does not own a projector, a model table, or anything you would call equipment. What it owns is a very old star chart pinned to a board the size of a door, a box of steel pins, and a spool of black thread. Ossul has been doing astrometry with a sewing kit for twenty two galaxals and it works, and I am not going to be the one who tells him it shouldn't.]

He pinned six worlds to the chart. Jötunheimr. Vædrun, which some of you will remember from Entry #2259, the ocean world where the tide goes out for nine days a click and never stops paying down a debt nobody wrote the end date on. And four more I had never given you, because I had never had a reason to: Thræsk, Ombrykt, Kaldris, Vyrn. All Category 3. All quiet worlds with quiet legends I have not yet found the space to tell you properly, and all six, it turns out, with something buried, carved, or planted, and aimed.

Six pins. Six bearings. Ossul tied one end of a thread to each pin and stretched it out along its heading, off the edge of the board if it had to go that far, and pinned the loose end to a second board he keeps for exactly this, the one with nothing on it but distance.

He was three threads in when he stopped talking. I noticed because Ossul talks even less than I do, and he had gone all the way to silence.

The fourth thread was Vædrun's. I did not know Vædrun had a bearing on file at all. It is not in anything I filed in Entry #2259, because I was not looking for one that day, I was busy with a tide and a burnt scroll and a woman gutting a fish who told me why they kept Sœbra in the pantheon. But the Sülm, it turns out, planted a line of stone counting poles along their high ground not long after the second Withdrawal, the one that had no proper festival, one pole for every clerk who has re-surveyed it since, aimed the same patient bureaucratic way the Hraedh aim their dead: not at the thing, at the way the thing went, on the reasoning that Vaal-Ne dried up somewhere, and somewhere is not nowhere.

Ossul pulled the thread taut and pinned it, and it crossed Skalde's line, the Jötunheimr line, the one aimed at four thousand clicks of empty sky, almost exactly on top of Vædrun's own pin.

He checked it with a ruler. I watched him check it with a ruler four times.

Then he went to prick the fourth pin back into its hole and put the pin straight through the pad of his thumb instead, because his hand was not steady, and he said "sorry, hold on" in the same flat clerk's voice he uses for everything, and put his thumb in his mouth for a second like a much younger man, and then went back to the board and did not mention it again.

The other two pairs came apart the same way once we knew to look. Thræsk's line meets Ombrykt. Kaldris meets Vyrn. Not roughly. Not within a generous tolerance I am being kind about. Within the same third of a degree the Hraedh masons cut their own stone to, on a scale of actual light years, on worlds that have never traded a signal, using instruments none of them know the others own.

Here is what I think happened, and I want to say plainly that this is the first time in this whole series I have been confident enough in a mechanism to call it a mechanism instead of a guess. A monster driven off a world does not stop existing. It goes somewhere. The somewhere, on every one of these six worlds, turned out to be populated. Skalde left Jötunheimr in a direction, and the direction had a world in it, and the world it had in it was Vædrun. And Vædrun built its own fence, quietly, patiently, aimed at wherever its own Vaal-Ne went when the tide finally ran it dry. And what Vaal-Ne went toward, when I finally made myself check, is Jötunheimr.

I am the first entity in the recorded history of the galaxy to see all six of these bearings on one table at the same time. I did not feel anything you would call satisfaction when Ossul told me that, and I want to be honest about that rather than write you a better sentence. I have been chasing a fourteen count across nine worlds for a season and a half hoping it would add up to something, and it has just added up to something, and it turned my stomach.

The Hraedh have been aiming four billion graves at Vædrun for four thousand clicks. And Vædrun has been aiming back the same patient way, one counting pole at a time, for nearly as long. Neither world knows. Both of them think they are watching empty sky, on the reasonable and until eleven days ago entirely correct assumption that empty sky is what a fence watches. Thræsk and Ombrykt are doing it to each other. So are Kaldris and Vyrn. Six worlds, six fences, three old wars, and every single one of them thinks it is alone in the dark keeping a promise to a direction, when the direction has a name and an address and, in one case, four billion people also awake at three in the morning maintaining their half of the same watch.

I sat with that for a while in Ossul's second chair, the one he never explains, and I asked myself the honest question, which is whether I tell them.

I did this once already, on Threnn, and told myself that one did not count because there was nothing anyone on Threnn could have done with what I knew even if I had said it. I do not have that excuse here. Vædrun and Jötunheimr could, in principle, send a message along a bearing they already own the exact heading for. It would not be hard. It would take one field officer with slightly better manners than me and about a click of travel time, and two civilisations who have spent four thousand clicks each fencing off a threat would find out, for the first time, that the thing on the other side of the fence has a face and a name and a tallykeeper of its own.

I have not told them. I noticed, sitting there, that this is the second time in this case I have chosen the same silence, and I noticed it the way you notice a stair you have already tripped on once, with my foot already most of the way into the same step. I did not stop my foot. I want to be honest about that too. I closed Ossul's second board, the one with nothing on it but distance, and I asked him not to file the coordinates as a matched pair. He did not ask me why. He did not write anything down. And neither, for once, is this page going to tell you what I decided, because I have not decided it. I have just gone on saying nothing, one more click, same as everyone else in every legend I have ever filed for you.

Is there truth in what six threads on an old man's sewing board add up to? I checked it four times myself before I believed it, and I am still not sure that counts as proof. It is the closest I have ever come to measuring one.
