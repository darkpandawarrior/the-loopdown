# The Thing I Did Not Say On Threnn

> Kindling · page 23 withdrawn

Greetings again, my dear readers, page twenty three, out of the case and into my hand and on its way to the fire, and I will tell you plainly this is the one I have been dreading since before I built the ninety one slots, though I did not know that at the time, because dread is patient and does not send word ahead.

This is the Threnn page.

You already know the rule if you were with me for it the first time. A thing can only harm you if you have not said it out loud. Every Ovai on that wet green world grows what they call a Kept out of exactly whatever they have swallowed, and grows out of nothing else, and they talk constantly, to fruit sellers and children and, I am reliably informed, livestock, as a matter of what I can only call civic hygiene. It is the most honest civilisation I ever filed and I could not stand two ticks of it, which tells you something about the man writing this and I am not going to pretend otherwise on the page where pretending is the entire subject.

Ÿssa asked me twice. Once over a meal I never did learn the name of, and once at the transit dock, quietly, not in front of anyone, which I noticed at the time and am noticing again now with the page in my other hand. Whatever I was carrying, she said, she would sit with however long it took me to say it badly, because everybody says the first one badly.

I said no both times.

And here is the part I did not write down two seasons ago and am apparently about to write down now, which is either brave or the last kind of cowardice, the kind that only shows up once nobody who could actually hear it is in the room. I have the sentence half formed. I have had it half formed since the transit dock lifted off. It starts, near as I can get it after everyone since, with

Well.

It starts.

That is as far as I am going to take you, my dear readers, and I want to be honest that this is not artistry, this is just where the sentence stops for me too, on the page, with the fire already going in the grate and my hand already over the slot it came from. Ÿssa would have a term for this, probably an unkind one, delivered kindly, the way she delivered everything. I do not have hers. I only have mine, and mine has never once made it all the way to the end of itself, not on Threnn, not in a wooden case, not here.

[Kindling Fact- I tried, before starting this page, to recall the name of the boy at the fruit stall, the one who took his mother's apology like it was the price of the fruit. I was certain I had written it down. I have looked. It is not on any page I still have, and it was never anywhere else, and I cannot get it back, and I want to be clear that I noticed this the way you notice a draft, not the way you notice a wound, and I am going to keep writing anyway]

That is the whole of it, and I am aware, more aware than I have been about anything on any of these pages so far, that this is the one page in the entire case where the cure was sitting across the table from me with an offer already made, twice, and all it required of me was the same thing every Ovai on that planet does before breakfast without thinking of it as brave. I did not do the easy thing on the one world built entirely to make it easy. I am about to burn the only written record that I almost did, and the sentence inside it is going to go into the fire exactly as unfinished as it has been since I carried it off that planet, and I know, setting it down, precisely how that reads.

I am doing it anyway.

The page catches slower than the last one did. Wet paper burns slower, and I do not know why this particular page still smells faintly of somewhere green, three seasons and however many milgalaxals of ship time later, but it does, right up until it does not, and then Threnn is thirteen unnamed people, one rule, and a woman who asked twice, and nothing else, anywhere, that remembers she asked.

Is there truth in their legend? No one knows. My aim is never to prove or disprove the tales I come across. Just to report them to you, my dear readers, on the pages where I still can.

This is Lu'kifær Morkinstar. Page twenty three, withdrawn.
