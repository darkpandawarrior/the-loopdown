# The Interloop

## Thesis
The Interloop. The record is never finished being read. Each season is a rendering of the seasons before it, and a rendering can hold, strain, fail, or refuse; when a new season lands, what an earlier one meant moves, and what it cost does not. A later season may reprice a page. It may never refund one. The seasons therefore work separately, together, or in any order, because an account with an unwritten page in it is not complete, and a thing that is not complete cannot be Concluded.

## The medium loop
Closed, and closed for a real reason, not a neat one. The four media are four relations between the record and its reader: broadcast is read by everyone; never sent is read by no one he can name (canon then reveals the case read itself); destroyed is unreadable; executed is run without being read at all. Execution is the return leg because a schema is a broadcast with the reader removed: it reaches everyone, constantly, forever, with the authorship stripped, so Season Four is Season One at industrial scale, and that is the horror. Canon already supplies the proof of medium: a record that is read can be argued with, and his corrections were acknowledged and not actioned, which is what being read looks like; in Season Four the corrections are actioned in bulk as a dataset purchase, which is not an argument, it is an ingestion, and the fare gate plays his disclaimer as a recorded courtesy, which is a sentence being executed rather than heard. The loop is also closed causally, not just thematically: the broadcast could never be burned (the fire proved it by only being able to touch the never-sent case), so the broadcast aged for one Vænheim and became the thing that gets executed. And the media interlink inside Season Four, which is the interloop's strongest form: the wall is the case at civic scale (accumulation, nothing destroyed, only covered), the city is the broadcast poured in concrete, and the fire exists as a costed proposal, priced and declined in the public accounts. Season Four does not follow the three prior media; it contains them as infrastructure. One condition, non-negotiable: the closure is only real under Fable's revision. If piece fourteen's free Kindling stands as fact, paint equals fire, Season Four contains a working Season Three at zero cost, the media collapse into one another, and the loop is not a loop, it is a drain.

## Reading orders

### The Ratchet (1, 2, 3, 4 (publication order, which is also chronological))
The reader repeats his error at every season boundary: files the season as complete, and the next one reopens it. The retroactions land as detonations, the dread counters work as dread, and being ahead of him hurts. This is the default and the site must keep it the default.

### The Excavation (4, 3, 2, 1)
The anthology read as the wall: top layer first, then down through the torn corners. The disclaimer, met first as a fare gate's recorded courtesy, is read last as a young man's sincere sentence, which is the crueller direction. Warmth arrives last, and S1's entries read as the fragments legible under the paint.

### The Audit (4, then 1, 2, 3)
Total dramatic irony. Every S1 correction 'acknowledged and not actioned' reads as a purchase order in draft; every page added to the case in S2 is watched by a reader who already knows what walls do with pages; the count of fourteen is not a mystery but a calibration error the reader has already lived inside.

### The Fire First (3, then 1, 2, 4 in any arrangement)
Grief before context. The reader watches a man burn pages that mean nothing to them, then spends three seasons acquiring the meaning of what they watched go. The five unseen burned pages stop being gaps and become the reader's own losses: they know exactly as much about those worlds as the fire left.

### Any Season Alone (any single season, standalone)
The Rendering applied to reading itself. A partial read is a rendering that resolved toward the familiar, and it holds: S1 alone is a warm travelogue with a ratchet, S2 alone a horror of accumulation, S3 alone a chamber piece about grief, S4 alone a civic satire. Each is complete, none is wrong, and the variance is canon, not inconsistency. Honest limit: these five are the orders that are actually different experiences; the other permutations are shufflings of these and the site must not enumerate twenty-four.

## The Season Three question
Two proposed breaks, two different verdicts. First, the synthesis as written: piece fourteen's free Kindling, stated as fact, does break Season Three's ending, exactly as Fable ruled. It resolves 'did the fire work' downward and refunds the fire's cost, and s3-bible forbids both ('Do not resolve whether it worked'; every genuine unweighting took something of him out with the page). Under Fable's revision (he is wrong, the season knows it, paint is not fire), Season Four does not break Season Three: it repeats S3's founding error at civic scale, a man copying the wrong half of a ritual a second time, while the accreditation file (a survey with no end date cannot close) is the blank page's logic still running one Vænheim later. Verdict: permitted only with the revision applied; the revision is not optional, it is the interloop's load-bearing wall. Second, the duplicate: 'the archive was sold before Season Three, so the fire destroyed a duplicate' is REFUSED, because it is a retcon, not a retroaction, and canon triple-locks it. One: the object sold is Corpus 2300, the broadcast entries, and the object burned is the case, never sent; the two archives are distinct by construction and the fire never touched the corpus because a broadcast cannot be burned. Two: the Væhn rule requires every case page to contain something never written down anywhere, so the case is definitionally unduplicated; no licensed copy can contain it. Three: s2-09 states flatly 'Nothing got aboard this ship, and nothing had to.' A licensed copy of the case would have to be invented against all three locks. And the found version is already better and already in the frame: he burned the only thing that was still his, and the thing he could never burn went on to build the city. Season Three's cost was never about Skerrin; species.md says the only thing he is, is the account, and the case was the only description of himself, so what went out with each page was memory, and memory is not licensable. The fire did exactly what it cost. What it could not do was reach the record he had already given away, and that failure is not a nullification of Season Three, it is the premise of Season Four. The governing law for this whole class, forever: a retroaction may change what a season meant, never what it cost.

## Site implications
- Tabs in publication order assert one order. Keep publication order as the default first-visit path, but render the four entry points as the four objects (the form, the case, the fire, the wall) rather than Season 1 through 4, so the media, not the sequence, are the navigation.
- Every season landing opens with one line that lets a reader start there. No season requires another as a prerequisite, no order-gating, no completion tracking, no recommended-order essay.
- The interloop's cross-linking mechanism already exists in canon and the site must reuse it rather than invent one: the damage register. S3 plates carry remnants of S1 and S2 plates, so an S2 page visited after S3 exists may carry its fate ('withdrawn, Kindling piece 6') as one line of small print in the Notice-conditions register at the foot, never as a banner, popup, or link card.
- Say 'works separately, together, or in any order' once, flatly, on /anthology, and never again anywhere.
- The site is itself a Season Four object: it keeps every season permanently, nothing is destroyed, the latest layer sits on top. Let the chrome admit it. The /anthology ground can be the wall, four layers on one surface with earlier chrome visible at torn corners, which is the S4 art doctrine used as information architecture, not a new invention.
- The counters (Concluded, Files Open, citations) render in site small print exactly as flatly as in the text and never animate. An animated dread counter is the dashboard the council already killed.
- Nothing in site chrome, metadata, excerpts, or link previews may finish #2300's sentence or 'clean' the mid-sentence ending. Auto-generated og descriptions and CMS excerpt trimming are the live risk: whoever fixes the truncation has deleted the ending.

## What this may not become
- A puzzle box. Every retroaction must land in publication order alone; no order unlocks content; a reader who never reorders misses nothing, they simply read one rendering, and it holds.
- An anthology that congratulates itself on being non-linear. The interloop is never advertised, explained, or performed; no narrator, character, or page of chrome names it; one flat line on the site and silence after.
- A series where nothing means anything. A retroaction moves meaning, never event, cost, or death. The phenomena stay checkable, the four species constraints stay fixed, the arithmetic still closes in every order. Chaos that is merely arbitrary is not chaos, it is noise, and the difference is that every reframe here carries a canon evidence line.
- Retroaction-first design. No future season may be designed by asking what it can reframe. Retroactions are found in shipped text during drafting or they are retcons and are cut. The three verified accidents are the standard: each was discovered on a page that already existed.
- Infinite mutual reframing. Each season carries a small number of load-bearing retroactions, each statable in one sentence with its evidence line quoted from a shipped file. A reframe without an existing evidence line does not exist.
- A clean win for the reader. No order of reading resolves whether the fire worked, who the readers are, what the fourteenth is called, or what he looks like. The open questions stay open in every order, or the interloop has failed at the only thing it is for.
