# The Morkinstar Journals — Anthology Bible

Ten entries. Six systems. One headcount.

Grown outward from `archive/legend-of-koaeluae-scales.md` (2021), which already had the
whole frame in it and did not know it was a series yet. Everything below was either
already on the page in that story or is the smallest honest extension of something that was.

**This is not The Loopdown's cosmology.** The Loopdown has a cast of engineering entities
(`lore/cast.md`, `lore/pantheon.md`) and a time-loop premise. This is a separate fiction.
Do not put the Concussed Witness in a Morkinstar entry and do not put K'öæluæ in a lesson.
They rhyme on purpose (a Power wearing different names per culture is the pantheon rule,
and it is here too), and the rhyme is the reward. Stating it out loud spends it.

---

## The frame

**Lu'kifær Morkinstar**, field correspondent for the **Galactic Directory**. Species
unstated, and stays unstated. Ship, translation rig, logbook. Files numbered **Journal
Entries**, each covering one world's legend and the natural phenomenon that legend explains.

Voice, inherited verbatim from the 2021 original:

- Opens *"Greetings again, my dear readers."* Signs off *"This is Lu'kifær Morkinstar
  concluding Journal Entry #NNNN, Series N of M from the X System."*
- The disclaimer is load-bearing and recurs almost word for word: *"Is there truth in
  their legend? No one knows. My aim is never to prove or disprove the tales I come
  across. Just to report them to you, my dear readers."* When it starts to bend, that
  is the arc.
- The joke lives in the parenthetical. `[Fun <Planet> Fact #N — …]` boxes. Direct address,
  rhetorical questions, self-deprecation, digressions he apologises for and then continues.
- Starred terms with a **Terminologies** block at the foot of every entry.
- **No em dashes anywhere in body prose.** Full stops, commas, colons, parentheses.
  House rule from `voice/voice-profile.md`; the 2021 original already obeys it.

## The Directory

A census, an employer, and eventually the subject. Its consistent characteristic across
all ten entries is that **it writes things down badly and nobody checks**. That is a
running gag for six entries and then it is the plot.

- **Categories** grade a species by reach. Category 3 = writing, no spaceflight. Every
  world in entries 1 to 9 is Category 3, because that is where the myths are richest.
- **Self-terms vs Directory terms.** The Directory records what a surveyor guessed.
  Morkinstar uses what the people call themselves. He files corrections. They are
  acknowledged and not actioned.
- Known Directory errors, all canon, all uncorrected: the Marltain binary filed as sex
  (#2263), the Killugan hold filed as *Greetings, physical* (#2269), the Væhn filed as
  *culturally conservative; low innovation drive* (#2284), and the milgalaxal conversion.

### The milgalaxal, and why it must never be fixed

The 2021 original states: *Milgalaxal — Equivalent to 2228 Hellheims or 2455 Earth Years*,
while a click is 1 Hellheim is 2 Earth years. 2228 × 2 = 4456. The line does not multiply out.

**This is the anthology's keystone and it is inherited, not invented.** Do not correct it
in any file. Entry #2300 explains it: the Hellheim was rebased when the world it was keyed
to was Concluded, every line in the table was restated, and that one was missed. By being
missed it became the last surviving record of that world's year. Morkinstar spent four
hundred entries trying to get it deleted.

## Canon: the seven laws

Every law is visible in the 2021 original. Nothing here was bolted on.

1. **The Count of Fourteen.** Every world's legend holds 14 gods and 14 monsters, and the
   Directory files 14 and 14, because the form has fourteen fields. Unconnected worlds,
   unrelated languages, identical census. Morkinstar notices at world **two** (#2250, out
   loud, and shrugs it off), starts the tally at world **three** (#2259), and stops joking
   at world **four**.

   **A census is a count. A pantheon is a document, and documents get amended.** Vædrun's
   has fifteen names on it, because the Sülm voted Sœbra on roughly 900 clicks after the
   fact over the priesthood's objection, and the Directory's file on Vædrun says fourteen,
   because there has never been a fifteenth field. Both are correct. Neither is the census,
   and the fourteen of the Standing are the entities with a front and a back (law six), which
   a clerk who lost a scroll is not. **This is the law's proof, not a hole in it: the one world
   that put its teller on the god list is the one world the record undercounts, by exactly
   one, forever.** Do not "fix" #2259. See [`error-doctrine.md`](error-doctrine.md) §5.
2. **The Unnamed Fourteenth.** Ask anyone to list the fourteen monsters and you get
   thirteen names and a pause. The pause is the most consistent phenomenon in the series.
   Reason revealed in stages: not ours (#2277) → its name is already on the god list
   (#2291) → it is the one that never split, so nobody has ever stood in front of it.
3. **The Halving.** A deadlock ends only when someone voluntarily divides themselves and
   spends both halves. K'öæluæ, Ösrun, Ombra, Anh-Rekk. Two deliberate breaks: Vædrun
   (nobody halved, so it never ended) and Jötunheimr (halving applied recursively, which
   converges to nothing).
4. **The Residue.** What is left over becomes the recurring phenomenon. The phenomenon is
   the receipt. Snow, silence, a nine-day tide, an eleven count, a line of graves.
5. **The Witness Who Tells It.** Every legend has one mortal, usually a woman, usually
   nobody important, who was present and told it afterwards. Feeriko, Tveggi, Sœbra,
   Sœlvi, **Ædri**, Hild-Ronn, the unnamed Cendran child, Ilta. The heroes lose. **The
   tellers are why there is a story at all**, and that is the anthology's thesis.

   This law is checkable and it was checked: the first draft of #2269 had no in-myth
   witness at all, only present-day Killugans talking *to* Morkinstar, which made this law
   false as written. Ædri was added and tied to the existing ledger of cold-holds. **If you
   write an entry and cannot name its teller, the entry is not finished.**
6. **The Two Facings.** *This is the Tvænd's account, not settled canon.* They say there
   were never twenty-eight: there were fourteen, each with a front and a back, and
   *hœl-ær* / *hœl-æth* is a locative describing where the speaker stood. Thirteen split
   when observed from both sides at once. One did not, because its front and its back are
   **the same face**, so there was nothing to separate and nothing to walk around to. Its
   single name sits on the god list, where people put it out of gratitude; it holds a line
   on the monster list with no name on it, because it only ever had the one.

   The arithmetic closes and Morkinstar does it on the page in #2291: thirteen splits give
   twenty-six names, plus the unsplit one is twenty-seven names filling twenty-eight lines
   across two lists. **Fourteen gods, fourteen monsters, thirteen monster-names and a pause.**
   Which is what every world reports. Show this working in any entry that leans on it; a
   reader who does the subtraction unaided gets 27 and concludes the book is broken.

   Keep it marked as one storyteller's position. Morkinstar himself refuses to close it
   (*"either the most elegant thing I have ever been told or a very old joke"*), and a
   future entry is free to contest it. The seams belong at the level of who is telling you.
7. **Concluded.** Directory status flag: *survey complete; no phenomena outstanding; no
   further contact indicated.* 611 worlds at #2296. 612 at #2300. All populated. All well.
   The Væhn child's word for the fourteenth renders as **Done**.

## The Rendering (added 2026-08-15, series-level)

Everything in this anthology reached the reader through **a translation rig and a Directory form**.
Both are established in the text: the rig "renders", it is argued with, its renderings vary
between tellings and sometimes fail; the Directory has spent sixty galaxals copying unverified
first impressions forward. **"Bipedal category species" is not an observation. It is a checkbox.**

So no description in thirty entries is a photograph. Every one is a rendering.

This is not a retcon. It is what the frame always was, and the Template Hypothesis is the proof:
the correspondent's great wrong idea was that the Directory pressed a familiar shape onto the
worlds it filed. He was wrong about the count of fourteen because he tested it on the **gods**.
**He never tested it on the bodies.**

Three things follow, and all three are load-bearing:

1. **The art may go as far as it likes.** A plate that resolves cleanly means the rig held. A
   figure half-dissolved into what it actually is means the rig strained. Hands in an unrendered
   void means it failed. **Ossul is a refusal**, because his species is never named anywhere in
   thirty entries and the instrument does not get to invent one.
2. **Variance between plates is canon, not inconsistency.** A teller who reads humanlike is a
   rendering that resolved toward the familiar, which is exactly what a Directory instrument
   would do, and the reader is entitled to distrust it.
3. **The correspondent has no body.** He is never described, never given a species, never named
   as anything, across the entire corpus. Everyone else at least got a rendering. He did not.
   **The only thing he is, is the account.** Season Three is built on that, and it is why burning
   the case is not an escape.

Full doctrine and the ten renderings: [`species.md`](species.md).

### Warmth, never contempt

Four cultures in a row go out of their way to tell Morkinstar their monster meant no harm.
Uhl is hungry. Skerrin is a consequence. Vaal-Ne is thirsty. Ottokh is the most reasonable
entity in his own legend. **Keep this.** It is not softness, it is the observation that the
people who survive long enough to have a legend are the ones who stopped needing a villain.

### No clean wins

Nine legends, not one victory. Ombra loses outright. Vör-Angi is divided into nothing.
Anh-Rekk becomes a fire that has to be relit every ninth generation. The Ombri have failed
for four thousand clicks and have not stopped. If a new entry ends in a win, it is wrong.

## The arc

Standalone entries. One ratchet running under them.

| # | Entry | World | Phenomenon | The turn |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | 2245 | Exxobar | snow | The frame. 14/14 stated for the first time. |
| 02 | 2250 | Grïnjdarlay | silence | Notices the count. Writing as a weapon. |
| 03 | 2259 | Vædrun | a nine-day tide | Starts the tally. The Halving named by absence. |
| 04 | 2263 | Marlt | no word for alone | Starts asking. Blank fourteenth line. |
| 05 | 2269 | Killuga Var | the eleven count | A child: *you don't name that one, you just check.* |
| 06 | 2277 | Jötunheimr | standing graves | *Not one of ours. It is on everyone's list.* |
| 07 | 2284 | Cendre | the ninth-generation burn | Reads the name. Withholds it. First rule broken. |
| 08 | 2291 | Solvei | two suns, one shadow | Fourteen, not twenty-eight. Reframes 01 to 07. |
| 09 | 2296 | *[unnamed]* | none | A finished world. Status: Concluded. 611 of them. |
| 10 | 2300 | The Directory | time named after hells | Turns the method on his employer. Will not conclude. |

**Entry numbers are global; series positions are per-system.** He travels, so gaps are
normal and deliberate. Do not make them contiguous.

**The constraint that follows, and it is checkable.** Entry numbers are unique and
sequential, so within one system a series position can rise by at most 1 per entry number.
If #2250 is "7 of 16", then "16 of 16" cannot arrive before #2259. This caught a real error
in the first draft (Vædrun was filed as #2258, which was unreachable) and it will catch the
next one. **Run the arithmetic before you assign an entry number.**

### Form as payload

Three entries break their own shape on purpose and the break is the point.

- **#2284** loses its jokes as it goes and states so: *there is no third Fun Cendre Fact
  in this entry. I had one. It was about the ink. I have taken it out.*
- **#2296** is 1,010 words with no legend, no Fun Fact, and a near-empty terminology block,
  because there was nothing there.
- **#2300** is **filed incomplete.** It ends mid-sentence at *"I am not going to"*. This is
  not a truncated file. It is the last move in the anthology: the Væhn keep one page and it
  must always be something new, so the record is never complete, and a thing that is not
  finished cannot be Concluded. **Never finish that sentence.** Anyone who "fixes" it has
  deleted the ending.

## Writing entry eleven and beyond

The series is open by construction. If it continues:

1. **One world, one phenomenon, one legend, one witness.** If you cannot state the
   phenomenon in a single line a Directory clerk would file, there is no entry.
2. **The phenomenon must be checkable.** Snow, a tide, a silence, a burial angle. Something
   an instrument could argue with. Morkinstar's whole credibility is that he measures.
3. **14 and 14, thirteen names, one pause.** Non-negotiable. It is the spine.
4. **Somebody halves, or the entry has to say out loud that nobody did.** See Vædrun.
5. **Name the teller.** Not the hero. The teller.
6. **The Directory has got something wrong about this world**, and Morkinstar finds it, and
   files a correction, and it is acknowledged and not actioned.
7. **He does not conclude any entry after #2300.** The sign-off is gone. Whatever replaces
   it has to be something that stays open.

### Naming

Diacritic-dense, Norse and Sanskrit blended with invented shapes, apostrophes for glottals.
`æ œ ö ï ë ÿ`. Gods get two syllables and hard consonants (Ösrun, Ottokh, Ombra, Skalde,
Anh-Rekk). Monsters get one (Uhl, Grin, Skerrin, Vaal-Ne). Self-terms are always a plain
descriptive phrase and usually a slightly sad one: *the counted*, *those who wait*, *the
standing*, *those who begin*, *the turned*.

### The morpheme

`ær` / `æth` appears on Marlt (first-drawn / second-drawn) and on Solvei (from in front /
from behind), two systems apart, in unrelated languages. Morkinstar records it and refuses
to theorise about it, correctly, because one coincidence is not a pattern. Do not explain
it. If a later entry finds a third instance, that is a whole entry on its own.

## Design

`scripts/morkinstar-plates.mjs` renders all ten field plates plus a series cover into
`assets/`. Run `node scripts/morkinstar-plates.mjs` for everything, or pass an entry
number (`07`) for one.

The plate is the Galactic Directory's own survey form, which is the joke: an institution
that produces immaculate documentation of things it has not understood. Chrome is
byte-identical on all ten. **Only the middle changes.**

- Each illustration draws the **mechanism**, not a mood. A reader who has read the entry
  should be able to point at the part of the picture that is the twist.
- Entry 09's illustration is deliberately near-empty with a CONCLUDED stamp. The absence
  is the artwork. Do not fill it in later.
- The sigil in the footer bar is `sigil()` from `scripts/design-kit.mjs`, hashed from the
  entity's name. Rename the entity and the mark changes, because the mark was never the
  entity's, it belonged to the name. Same rule as `lore/pantheon.md`.
- Accents walk cold to warm to dead: ice blue → slate → sea → violet → orange → cyan →
  ember → rose → grey (09, the dead world) → **amber** (10). Amber is the house tape
  colour, the register that cannot lie. The finale gets it on purpose.
