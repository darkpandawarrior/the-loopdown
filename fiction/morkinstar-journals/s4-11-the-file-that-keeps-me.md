# The File That Keeps Me

> Posted · DEEP REGISTRY ANNEXE · cleared in 400 ticks

Greetings again, my dear readers.

The Galactic Directory is a district. I am going to give you that flatly and first, because I have been carrying it around for most of a momenta and it has not become any easier to say in a more complicated way.

It is not a ruin and it is not a monument and nobody has made a fuss of it. The ring is still a ring, or the signage says it is, and I will be honest with you that I could once see that for myself and cannot now, so I am taking it off a plaque like everybody else. The city grew onto it and then through it, and at some point the paperwork caught up and it became parcels with numbers on them, and there are people living nine decks down on the unlit side who think of it as home and have never once had occasion to use the word Directory in a sentence.

I came to look up my own accreditation.

I want to be honest about what I expected, because it turns out to be the whole entry. I expected it to have lapsed. I expected a line, and a date, and a code, and I thought that if I had the date I could put down a small background feeling I have been carrying since I arrived, which is the feeling of doing a job I am no longer entitled to do, in a city that has no field for me, with a brush and a folded sheet. I wanted the lapse. I had it half written. I was going to be quite good about it.

Deep Registry itself is behind glass.

The corridor is listed. There is a plaque at the head of it, in the city's own lettering, and it says DEEP REGISTRY (FORMER), and under that LISTED, and under that a schedule of what is being preserved, which reads: CORRIDOR · DOOR · DESK · CHAIR · LAMP · SHELVING, 2 BAYS · ILLUMINATION LEVEL. Then a byelaw number, then a revision date, then nothing else at all.

The last item on that list is the one I want to give you properly, because it is the sort of thing this city does without breaking stride. The corridor is dark. That is not neglect and it is not atmosphere, it is fabric. Deep Registry was on the unlit side, so the darkness is part of what was listed, at a stated level, with a tolerance of nothing whatever in the upward direction. So the dark is maintained. There is a card on a hook by the door and somebody comes every quarter with an instrument and confirms that the dark is still to standard and initials the card, and that card has been initialled for a very long time, in several hands.

There is a coffee concession twenty paces along. Its lamp has a hood on it and the hood is a condition of the licence, which is stencilled on the leg of the counter as B/38 and explained by nobody.

[Also posted- I asked the woman at the concession whether the hood was a nuisance. She said it was a nuisance for the first two years and is now simply the shape of the lamp, and then she asked me if I wanted anything, which I did not, and she wished me luck downstairs anyway, and I have been thinking about the fact that she said downstairs rather than down there.]

The working archive is two tiers below all of that, and it is not dark and it is not listed. It is a counter under strip lighting with a ticket dispenser at one end and a bell at the other and a floor that has been mopped recently and smells of it. That is the Annexe. Nobody photographs the Annexe.

The person behind the counter is called **Danvik**.

He has a hand lamp clipped at his collar, turned right down, and he keeps it angled away from whoever he is speaking to. Not aimed at the counter, exactly. Aimed at the counter's near edge, so that the light lands on the paper and goes nowhere else. It is not a courtesy he has to think about. He works two tiers under a corridor that is legally required to be dark and the habit has gone all the way in. He did it for me as well.

I told him what I wanted. He asked me for a name and I gave him one, and he asked whether I had a reference and I said no, and he said that was fine, most people do not, that is what the counter is for.

Then he went away and came back with a chair. He set it at the end of the counter and turned back to the terminal.

He found it in about six minutes without hesitating once, which included going down the row twice and pulling one physical manifest off a shelf to check a code against, and he narrated the whole thing while he did it in the manner of a man who has learned that people standing at a counter would rather hear a search than watch one. He passed me the slip with the reference on it, with the flat of his lower arm, and went back to the screen.

Then he said, in the same voice, that it had not lapsed.

I asked him to say that again. He said it again, in exactly the same words, which is what people do when they are certain, and here is what he told me, and he was right, and I am going to give it to you the way he gave it to me.

There are two classes of file that can carry an accreditation. There is a personnel file, which belongs to a person and to the body that employs them, and which closes when the post closes, on a date, cleanly, the way a thing ought to. And there is a survey file, which belongs to a survey. An accreditation issued against a survey lives inside that survey, because the accreditation is what the survey authorised, and a survey file cannot be closed while the survey is open. That is not a policy anybody is defending. It is the plain sense of it. You do not close the folder while the work in the folder is still being done.

Mine is a survey file.

It should not be. Danvik was careful about that and said so twice: nothing in the record is irregular from where he sits, a survey file coded as a survey file is a survey file, and he has no basis on his side of the counter to think otherwise. But he did say, without being asked, that the coding was done a very long time ago, and that in that period an accreditation was frequently issued on the same sheet as the survey it authorised, and that when a sheet holds two things, whoever files it has to pick one.

Somebody picked. I would like to say I remember them. I do not remember them and I have stopped pretending to myself that I might. What I remember, and I am fairly confident about this, is the form: a single sheet, in landscape, with the survey across the top and the authorisation underneath it in a box, and it did not have an end date field on it at all, because at the time it was drawn up nobody was surveying anything that was expected to still be going.

The end date field was added afterwards, in a later version, and every record already in the drawer was carried across into it, and the ones that had no end date on the sheet came across with the field empty.

Mine is empty.

Danvik showed me the permitted values, because I asked, and because you can just ask. The field takes a date, or it takes the value CONTINUING, and CONTINUING is a positive statement made by somebody with the authority to make it, and it has to be reviewed. Empty is neither. Empty is not a value at all, it is the absence of one, and a record with a required field empty cannot be validated, and a record that cannot be validated cannot be closed, and a survey that cannot be closed is open.

So I am still a field correspondent for the Galactic Directory, my dear readers, and have been continuously and without interruption for a length of time I am not going to work out in front of you, because of a sheet of paper in landscape and a schema change.

I asked who holds it.

Nobody holds it. That was the part I had to stand still for. The post that used to sit at the bottom of Deep Registry was consolidated, and then the consolidation was consolidated, and there is no successor and no queue with a person in it. Danvik was neither apologetic nor pleased about this and I would not have wanted him to be either. He said the Annexe holds it in the sense that the Annexe holds everything, and that holding is not the same as attending to.

I have seen a thing like this once before, nine decks up and a long way back, kept alive by a man who declined to action four requests for twenty two galaxals and never told anybody, on the reasoning that the safest place for a number is a queue nobody reads. He was protecting something. He had decided to, and he kept deciding to, every time it came round.

This has nobody. It is not being protected. It survives the way a stone survives in a wall.

And here is the part I owe you, and I am going to put it plainly and not dress it, because I have not been able to make it sound better in four days of trying.

I did not tell him it was in the wrong drawer.

I could have. It would have taken one sentence. I know what the sheet looked like and I know which of the two things on it was the accreditation, and Danvik would have taken it seriously, because he takes everything seriously, and he would have raised it, correctly, and somebody would have recoded it as a personnel file, and a personnel file whose employing body was consolidated closes. That is the whole of it. One sentence at a counter, and a lapse date, and the thing I came down here to get.

I know exactly whose move that is. I have written about him admiringly. I made it for myself, in a mopped room under strip lighting, and I did not enjoy it, and I am not going to tell you I have decided anything about it, because I have not.

What Danvik did do, in front of me, at the counter, saying each part of it out loud as he went, was raise the record as unvalidated. That is his job. A file in the Annexe that cannot be validated is reported, and reporting it puts it on the register, and the register is the one that gets published, and he told me that before he did it and asked if I understood, and I said I did.

I asked what happens next.

He said somebody fills the field. I asked when. He said when there is somebody whose job it is.

The notice panel is at the head of the listed corridor, by the door to the stair, and it is resurfaced on a four hundred tick cycle, which is a conservation operation and is carried out by a different department from the one that does walls. Danvik told me that without my asking, and told me which department, and that they are very good.

I wrote up the Annexe. Where it is, which stair, what hours, what you need to bring, which is nothing, and the plain sentence that you are entitled to ask for your own file and that most people do not know that. Danvik said most people do not know that. It is two thirds of a sheet and there is nothing about me on it.

I put it up under the hooded lamp with the light angled at the near edge of the paper and going nowhere else, because he came up and held it for me while I worked.

The bottom corner lifted. I put my thumb on it and held it there until it stopped.
